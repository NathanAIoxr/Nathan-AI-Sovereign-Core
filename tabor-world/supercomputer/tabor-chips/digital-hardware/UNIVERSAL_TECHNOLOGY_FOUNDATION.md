## Four fundamental elements

1. **Physical atoms, signals, energy and heat:** semiconductor materials, conductors, photonics, radio, electrical power, thermal constraints, and manufacturing.
2. **Binary switching and logic:** digital representations, Boolean algebra, state machines, registers, arithmetic, instructions and control. Binary 0/1 are logical states; physical encoding may use voltage ranges, charge, magnetic states or optical signals. Modern hardware includes analog and multi-level circuits.
3. **Four functional layers:** Input/Output, Compute, Memory & Storage, Control & Power.
4. **Deployment topology:** centralized data centers, distributed edge, local devices and hybrid networks.

The four mathematical operations are engineering workflow labels: **CLEAR 1−1=0; BUILD 1+1=2; INTEGRATE 1×1=1; VERIFY 1÷1=1.** They are not a substitute for Boolean logic, timing analysis, conservation laws, or physical performance measurements.

## Physical-to-digital mapping rules

Each component record must preserve: source identity; physical function; attributes and traits; I/O contract; compute behavior; state/memory behavior; power/clock/thermal constraints; digital counterpart type; deployment location; validation plan; continuation tag; provenance and measurements.

Digital models are programmable representations. They can simulate or emulate hardware behavior and connect to real hardware adapters; they do not automatically reproduce a physical device's throughput, energy or storage capacity.
