# DINÉ WITH PUMPKINS — EXPANDED OPPORTUNITY ARCHIVE
Status: brainstorming for Collyn's optional review, NOT a commercial commitment or a claim of tribal endorsement.

## Origin and verification
User describes the pumpkin supply as grown on Navajo Nation land in New Mexico. Verify actual farms, growers, land tenure, sourcing, and permissions with the company and growers before public claims. Diné is the people's own name; Navajo Nation is the sovereign tribal nation. Do not imply Diné people are one uniform historical culture, or solely a past-tense people.

## Educational foundation
Tell the history of Diné Bikéyah and Diné communities from Diné-authored, tribally appropriate sources. Explain that labels such as 'savages' were historically dehumanizing, rather than adopt the label. Include the Long Walk (Hwéeldi), forced removal, return, treaty history, sovereignty, resilience, contemporary communities, arts, agriculture, language, and stewardship. Seek paid Diné editorial review and permission for restricted knowledge, images, recordings, stories, names and designs. Do not equate peacefulness or relationship with nature with a simplistic stereotype. Do not invent translations or use tribal insignia without authorization.

## Food, cookbook and agricultural opportunities
Diné with Pumpkins cookbook (only with permission and properly credited contributors); 101 Lost Pumpkin Recipes; CPT Chestnuts, Pumpkin and Potatoes; Three Sisters corn/beans/squash garden kits and classes; pumpkin seed and squash cooking; community-contributed recipes; seasonal meal plans; family heritage cookbooks; kids' recipe cards; farm-to-table education; water and soil conservation; seed saving; pollinator learning; desert agriculture; youth gardening badges; seed catalogs and planting calendars. Clearly distinguish Diné-specific documented traditions from broader Indigenous, regional and modern recipes. Obtain recipe/photo licenses and contributor compensation.

Paid Diné growers, suppliers, teachers, authors, artists, and cultural reviewers; licensing and royalties; sourcing transparency; partner-approved product revenue sharing; school and church educational kits; year-round farm/garden products; potential farm visits only with explicit permission and safety review. Do not claim an official Navajo Nation partnership, charitable endorsement, cultural authority, or guaranteed proceeds. Review tribal sovereignty, intellectual property, publicity, privacy and any applicable commercial rules.
