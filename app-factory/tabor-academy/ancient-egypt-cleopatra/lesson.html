Forward · Giza and the people who built it

Begin at Heit el-Ghurab. Excavators found a planned settlement, food supply remains and an adjacent cemetery. The settlement is associated chiefly with Khafre and Menkaure, with a possible earlier phase. Next, read Inspector Merer’s log from Khufu’s reign. It records a crew shipping limestone from Tura toward Khufu’s pyramid complex. Together the sources document skilled, organized crews, provisioning and royal logistics. Rations establish material support. An inference about wages, a worker’s freedom to leave, or an annual flood-season schedule requires evidence specific to that claim.

Keep Giza’s Fourth Dynasty crews distinct from military captives and other forms of servitude across Egyptian history. Compulsory service, dependent labor and enslavement had different legal meanings across periods. Egyptian documents should be dated before generalizing about inheritance, marriage, property or debt.

Forward · Cleopatra VII in the record of her reign

Start with the ruler’s public record. Compare Cleopatra’s royal titles on coins with her representation with Caesarion at Dendera. Then read P.Bingen 45, a royal tax exemption from 33 BCE. It records an act of government. The attribution of the handwritten approval to Cleopatra personally is a scholarly proposal. Plutarch’s later biography describes her multilingual diplomacy and says earlier Ptolemaic kings had neglected Egyptian.

Read the medieval Arabic tradition in its own right. Al-Masudi presents Cleopatra as a philosopher who honored scholars. This preserves a different reputation. Attributions of medical and alchemical texts require separate checks on date, transmission and which Cleopatra is meant.

Reverse · Trace the story back to a source

  1. “Slave-built Egypt.” Read Herodotus, Histories 2.124–125. His account of Khufu compelling Egyptians to work comes roughly two millennia after the pyramids. Compare the account with Merer’s contemporary work log. What does the later writer add? What question could neither source answer on its own?
  2. “Seductress Cleopatra.” Read Plutarch 58 and Cassius Dio 50.3–4 beside the coins and ordinance. The later accounts describe Octavian obtaining and publicizing Antony’s will and report a burial provision in Alexandria. The alleged transfer of Rome’s capital requires separate evidence. At Actium in 31 BCE, compare the ancient accounts with Barry Strauss’s reconstruction of a planned breakout. Identify what was observed, what was reported, and what the historian inferred.
