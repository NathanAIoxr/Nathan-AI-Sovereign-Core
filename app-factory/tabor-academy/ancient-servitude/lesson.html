Mesopotamia · Laws of Hammurabi §§117–119

Section 117 specifies three years of debt service and release in the fourth for the people covered by that provision. Read §§118–119 alongside it for different rules involving people already enslaved. Who could pledge another person’s labor? Which provision gives a release date? A release date can exist in a coercive arrangement.

Egypt · Labor, dependence and servitude across periods

Egyptian terms such as bꜣk and ḥm appear in records of service and dependence. The rights and obligations depend on the period and document. Compare compulsory work, household service, and captivity in Loprieno’s overview, then read the study of Late Period debt to see how scholars test whether a document actually establishes that default led to bondage.

Reverse · Start with “slave,” “servant,” or “worker”

  1. Record the date, place, original term, and who produced the document. Identify whether it is a law, a contract or a record of something that happened.
  2. Write down how service began, who could refuse or leave, what the text says about food or pay, and what it says about family, property and transfer to another household.
  3. Choose an English description and point to the clause that supports it. A broad claim such as “most often voluntary” or “usually three to seven years” requires a representative body of records across places and periods.

Check your comparison

Food and lodging establish what was supplied. A right to quit, a fixed release date, and a power to pledge or transfer another person are distinct facts. To connect this exercise to The Time of Peace, identify who acquired authority over someone else’s labor and what the record says about how that authority ended.
