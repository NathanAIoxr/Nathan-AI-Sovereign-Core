Rockefeller, Common Core and the money trail

John D. Rockefeller funded the General Education Board, founded in 1902. Frederick T. Gates wrote The Country School of To-morrow for the board in 1913. Read what he expected rural schools to teach and decide whose goals those schools would serve. The grants and proposal show an attempt to shape education. A literal purchase of the public school system would require ownership or control records.

Bill Gates’s foundation helped finance Common Core, and its chief acknowledged implementation mistakes in 2016. RAND evaluated a separate teacher-effectiveness initiative involving evaluation and staffing. RAND counted $575 million across seven sites, including about $212 million awarded by the Gates Foundation, and found little improvement in key student outcomes. That total belongs to the teacher initiative. The available evidence did not establish a $500 million Gates payment for Common Core math. Who had the power to change a standard? Check the state’s adoption and revision records. Florida’s move to B.E.S.T. is one documented example.

What sits beside the school ladder?

  • Early learning includes early intervention, preschool and pre-K. Kindergarten is the K in K–5.
  • Elementary, middle or junior high, and high school have locally varying grade boundaries. K–8 and K–12 are school configurations. Gifted education, special education, dual enrollment and career and technical education can run alongside grade levels.
  • After high school, routes include apprenticeships, trade programs, certificates, community college, associate and bachelor’s degrees, graduate degrees, and professional training. Adults may return through adult basic education, equivalency programs and continuing education.
