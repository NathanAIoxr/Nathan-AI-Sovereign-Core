The 1998 thesis

Nathan Tabor recalls submitting a thesis on privatizing the public school system at Regent University in 1998. His account opens with a question about who knows the child and who decides how that child learns. Start with the child, family, and teacher, then trace outward to the school, district, state, federal grant, and any private organization with a role in the decision.

Who decides what gets taught?

Federal law limits direct federal control of curriculum. Federal money can still carry conditions that influence what states do. An academic standard specifies what a student should be able to do. A curriculum specifies what is taught and in what sequence. A teacher’s lesson plan specifies how a particular class will learn it.

Common Core · Who wrote it, funded it, and adopted it?

The National Governors Association and the Council of Chief State School Officers led development of the standards released in 2010. The Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation was a major funder. States made adoption and revision decisions, while the federal Race to the Top competition created incentives for common standards. Follow each decision to its own record. For a claim that a particular person profited, trace the award, recipient and any documented financial interest.

Follow one lesson and one dollar

  1. Pick the same math skill in a California school and a North Carolina school. Compare each state’s standard, the materials each district selected, and how a teacher actually teaches it.
  2. Pick one grant or contract. Record who appropriated the money, who awarded it, who received it, what conditions applied, and who could change the decision.
  3. Compare public operation, a privately managed charter, and private tuition assistance. For each, ask who can enroll, who sets the curriculum, what services reach the child, and who answers when the school fails to deliver.
