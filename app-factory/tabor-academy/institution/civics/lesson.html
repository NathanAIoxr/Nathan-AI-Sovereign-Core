Diné and “We the People”

Read the Navajo Nation’s Fundamental Laws in the Nation’s own publication. Begin with Kʼé, the kinship system addressed in the customary-law section. Trace how the text speaks about mutual responsibilities and the land. Read the U.S. Constitution’s Preamble, then compare how Article I describes apportionment, including the original three-fifths formula. Keep the political and legal setting of each source attached to its words.

Use a two-column chart. For each source, record whose voice is speaking, who has an obligation, what the source says about belonging or representation, and where the text gives your evidence.

The Long Walk and the 1868 treaty

Trace the sequence from forced removal and confinement at Bosque Redondo to the June 1, 1868 treaty at Fort Sumner. Read Article II on the reservation, Articles XI and XII on the return and federal assistance, and Article XIII on settlement restrictions. Read Article VI of the U.S. Constitution on the status of treaties. Write down one specific commitment, the party responsible for it, and the additional historical record you would need to find out how it was carried out.

Lesson sequence and mastery

  1. 5 minutes · Warm-up. Write one example of pursuing something and one of obtaining it.
  2. 10 minutes · Direct instruction. Compare the two declarations and underline the verbs.
  3. 15 minutes · Guided practice. Make the two-column chart using the Navajo Nation’s own text and the U.S. Constitution.
  4. 15 minutes · Independent work. Choose a provision of the 1868 treaty and compare it with Article VI. Cite both passages and identify an implementation record to look for.
  5. 5 minutes · Exit check. Explain in three sentences what changes when “obtaining” is absent, where you found the words, and what the text leaves open.
