The Million-Dollar Multiplication Test

Start with $1,000,000 actually held, and write $1,000,000 × 0 = $0. The product is $0. The original balance is still $1,000,000 if the only event was writing the equation. Use Nathan Tabor’s Nine Cars Axiom and Three Apples Reality to track the difference between a calculation and a change in the world.

1 + 1 = 10 and 1 + 1 = 2

Put two physical counters on the table. Decimal records the count as 1 + 1 = 2. Binary records that count as 1 + 1 = 10₂. In binary, the left digit represents one group of two and the right digit represents zero ones. Both displays count the same two counters. In decimal, 10 represents ten. Put the base and unit on every record before subtracting or comparing.

Trace the 80% before payout

Start with Nathan’s 80% as an audit scenario. If a $1,000,000 gross amount has a documented 80% taken out before payout, reconcile $800,000 taken out + $200,000 paid = $1,000,000 gross. In a real case, list every deduction in its own row. Record who received it or where it is held, what agreement or rule authorized it, and whether it was pending, settled, or reversed. Leave the recipient open when the evidence ends at the deduction.

Check that percentages use the same base. A 50% deduction from $1,000,000 and then a 30% deduction from the remaining $500,000 total $650,000, or 65% of the starting amount. Two deductions of 50% and 30% calculated on the original amount total $800,000. Ask for the base before adding percentages.

Conductor capstone · Who took my other one?

  1. Build · 1 + 1 = 2. Record the beginning balance, gross amount, unit, base, date, source locator, and named parties.
  2. Clear · 1 − 1 = 0. Remove unsupported inferences from the write-up. Preserve both the original record and any conflicting entry.
  3. Integrate · 1 × 1 = 1. Match both sides of every actual move. Separate cash, securities, valuation, charges, and amounts marked ineligible.
  4. Verify · 1 ÷ 1 = 1. Recalculate from the original amount. Name every documented destination, mark unresolved amounts, and state who needs follow-up under Always Protecting The One.

Worked reconciliation. In the $1 million exercise, a 0% fee leaves $1 million. An actual 80% deduction leaves $200,000 after $800,000 is accounted for. The 80% line remains a model until the transaction records support the amount and identify the destinations.
