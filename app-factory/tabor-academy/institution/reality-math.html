Multiplication

1. The Nine Cars Axiom

In the system. If an event buys nine parking permits at $0 per permit, the total charge is $0. The multiplication is 9 × 0 = 0 dollars.

At the source. Count the cars. If nine cars are parked there, the physical count is nine cars. Check the unit next to each number before drawing a conclusion.

Practice. A receipt shows a $0 charge. A coworker says the lot must be empty. Write one sentence for the receipt, one for the car count, and one for what you still need to check.

Check your reasoning

“The receipt shows zero dollars charged. We counted nine cars in the lot at 10 a.m. Check whether the permit and headcount records refer to the same lot and time.”

2. When a multiplier hides a record

In the system. A report counts nine records only when an approval flag is 1. With the flag set to 0, the report counts zero approved records. Read the report title, its filter, and the approval rule.

At the source. Inspect the nine original records and the reason for their status. Record the number present, the number counted in that report, and what evidence would change the status.

Practice. A library dashboard shows zero published entries. The intake log shows nine drafts. What do you say to someone looking for a draft, and what changes would you request in the dashboard?

Check your reasoning

Say that nine drafts are recorded and zero are published. Ask the dashboard to label the count “published entries” and show an authorized reviewer where to find the drafts and their approval status.

3. When multiplication describes a real total

In the system. Three racks with space for three bicycles each give 3 × 3 = 9 spaces. The calculation describes capacity.

At the source. Inspect whether three racks are actually usable, then count the bicycles. Capacity and occupancy answer different questions.

Practice. Each rack has three spaces. One rack is closed and four bikes are present. Report design capacity, usable capacity, occupied spaces, and remaining usable spaces, assuming all four bikes occupy usable spaces.

Check your reasoning

Nine spaces by design, six usable spaces, four occupied, and two usable spaces remaining. Confirm that the closed rack is marked and the four bikes really are in the open racks before acting on the count.

All multiplication · More, less, and what matters

Apply that to all multiplication because sometimes you get more from your multiplying, but most of the time you get less, especially when it matters

Nathan Tabor

For a positive starting amount and a multiplier with no unit, compare the product with the original amount. Use the same unit on both sides.

MultiplierStart with 100What it does
Greater than 1100 × 1.25 = 125Increases the amount by 25.
Exactly 1100 × 1 = 100Keeps the amount the same.
Between 0 and 1100 × 0.8 = 80Keeps 80 of the 100. Work out what the other 20 represents.
Exactly 0100 × 0 = 0Makes the product zero. Check whether anything moved outside the calculation.
Below 0100 × (−0.5) = −50Flips the sign. Check what positive and negative mean for that record.

Starting at zero always gives zero. Starting below zero changes the ordering for positive factors. A $100 debt recorded as −$100 becomes −$125 after scaling by 1.25, so the amount owed grew by $25 while the signed balance fell. Multiplying a rate by a count changes units. At $3 per meal, 4 meals cost $12.

Practice with things people count on

  • Children · Food. Ten meals × 0.8 = 8 meals counted by the serving list. Put out ten counters, then move two to a reserve tray. Count eight available and two held, and name who can use the reserve.
  • Family · Time together. Four visits × 2 hours per visit = 8 scheduled hours. If three visits happen and one is canceled, compare the scheduled eight with the six delivered. Ask what happened to the missing visit and who can arrange a replacement.
  • Money · Earnings and take-home pay. Start with $100 and apply a 25% increase, then retain 80% at payout. $100 × 1.25 × 0.8 = $100. The first amount grew to $125. Trace the $25 difference before calling it a charge, a transfer, or a hold.
  • Ownership · More shares, price per share changes. In a hypothetical 2-for-1 split, 10 shares at $20 become 20 shares at $10 if prices adjust exactly in proportion. Both positions total $200. Follow the shares, per-share price, and cash separately.
  • Purchasing power · Money versus what it buys. If a fixed basket costs 25% more, the same money buys 1 ÷ 1.25 = 0.8 as many baskets. The balance stayed put while purchasing power fell for that basket.
  • Public program · Access. 100 seats × 0.6 available = 60 seats available today. Find whether the other 40 are filled, reserved, inaccessible, or uncounted. Keep each affected person visible in follow-up.

Measure “most of the time”

Choose one set of comparable records, such as a month of promised and received payouts or a term of promised and available seats. Record the dates, units, source, and outcome for every case. Count how often the recipient got less, and add how much less across the set. The frequency and the cumulative shortfall answer different questions.

Subtraction

4. Always Protect The One

In the system. A program had one active registration. One cancellation leaves 1 − 1 = 0 active registrations. Check the reason and who can correct the status.

At the source. The person may still arrive or need assistance. Ask what they need, confirm the cancellation directly when appropriate, and use the available correction or referral channel. Record the status and the human follow-up.

Practice. A learner arrives with a confirmation email, but the roster is empty after their registration was cancelled. Write the two facts you would record and the first action you would take.

Check your reasoning

Record “zero active registrations on today’s roster” and “one learner present with a confirmation email.” Contact the organizer to reconcile the records and help the learner find a place to wait or an alternative.

5. When something really leaves

In the system. One item in stock minus one item recorded as picked up leaves zero expected on-site stock.

At the source. Check the shelf, the pickup record, and the time. If the item is on the shelf, the ledger and observation disagree. If the pickup is confirmed, zero is an accurate count for that shelf after pickup.

Practice. The ledger says the last box was picked up at noon. You count one box on the shelf at 1 p.m. What would let you tell whether it is a duplicate, a return, or an incorrect entry?

Check your reasoning

Compare the box identifier, pickup receipt, return entries, and times. Until they reconcile, record one box observed and zero expected. Identify which result you are using to make the immediate decision.

6. A public copy and a primary record

In the system. Taking one entry out of a public index can move the visible count from one to zero. Determine whether the change affected visibility, access, or the underlying record.

At the source. Keep the original and its provenance in the appropriate archive. If a public copy omits private details, label the copy accordingly. Follow the record’s access rules when checking whether it still exists.

Practice. A public search returns zero results for a report after publication was withdrawn. What can you verify from that search? What can an authorized record keeper verify?

Check your reasoning

The search establishes zero results for that query at that time. An authorized record keeper can check the catalog and retention record to determine whether the original was retained, moved, or deleted.

Capstone · Reconcile the ledger with reality

A community event charged nine drivers $0 each. The dashboard says “0,” nine cars are in the lot, a volunteer was removed from the active roster but is present, and a report has disappeared from public search. Write a one-page handoff for the Library of Tabor Conductor.

  1. Write the exact system result with its number, unit, scope, and time.
  2. Write the direct observation and the source you used. Mark anything you have yet to corroborate.
  3. Say whether each discrepancy comes from different units, a different time or location, a filter or status, or a conflict about the same fact.
  4. Specify the next check and one action to help the person affected. Identify who can make a record correction and preserve the original entry alongside it.
Worked handoff and self-check

Nine drivers × $0 per driver = $0 charged. Nine cars were observed in the event lot at the stated time. The volunteer has zero active roster entries and is physically present. Confirm their role with the organizer and ask what support they need while the entry is checked. The report returns zero search results. Ask the authorized record keeper to verify its status before stating what happened to the original.

Check your own handoff for all four requirements above. If any sentence could change meaning when you add “in this report,” “at this time,” or “for this person,” make that scope explicit.
