Start with three apples. Move one. Count both places. Keep that same demonstration as the work grows from watching an object move to auditing records, reconciling balances, and accounting for a whole after it is divided.

Infancy · Watch it move

Caregiver activity. While the infant watches, move one large soft toy apple from your left hand to your right. Say “here,” then “there.” Repeat slowly and let the infant watch or reach. Use a soft toy with no detachable pieces.

What to notice. Watch for a shift of gaze or a reach toward the new location. Stop when the infant looks away. The adult models “same apple, new place.”

Toddler · Find the apple

Place three large toy apples on a mat. Move one into an open basket. Ask “Where did that apple go?” Have the learner point to it, bring it back, then move it to a different spot.

Check the demonstration

The learner can show the apple’s new location and return the same apple to the mat. The caregiver counts all three aloud.

Preschool · Two here, one there

Put three apples or counters on the table. Say “two minus one is one” and recount the table. Then physically move one into the basket and count each location. Use the words “table,” “basket,” and “altogether.”

Worked answer

The spoken equation leaves three on the table. The move leaves two on the table and one in the basket. There are three altogether.

Elementary · Same apples, different questions

Draw a box around the table. Write 3 − 1 = 2 for the table after the move. Draw a larger box around the table and basket and write 2 + 1 = 3 for both locations. Move the basket apple to a shelf and update both counts.

Worked answer

The table remains at two. The table and basket now contain two altogether. The table, basket, and shelf contain three altogether. Name the locations included before saying a total.

Middle school · Change the symbol, check the objects

Build two rows of three counters to show 3 + 3 = 6. Rotate the + on paper to make ×. Draw the three rows of three needed for 3 × 3 = 9, then identify what three counters would have to arrive to match that drawing. Apply the same check to nine cars and a $0 charge per car.

Division extension. Use a paper apple split into four equal-area quarters. Place one quarter on each of three plates and one in a reserve tray. Write the share per plate, the amount left in reserve, and the accounted-for total.

Worked answer

The new operation describes nine counters when there are three groups of three. Six remain on the table until a third group is supplied. Nine cars × $0 per car gives $0 charged. For the paper apple, 1 ÷ 4 = ¼ per piece, and ¾ across the plates + ¼ in reserve = 1 whole apple’s worth of paper.

High school · Follow the other one

A sample account starts at $2. A statement shows a $1 fee and an ending balance of $1. Ask your question from the handoff, “Who took my other one?” Make a table with the amount, date, transaction description, source, and recipient or counterpart entry.

A volunteer is removed from an event roster but arrives with a confirmation. Write what the roster says, what you observed, and how to help the person while the organizer checks the record. Apply Always Protecting The One.

Worked answer

The statement establishes a $1 deduction labeled as a fee. Record its destination as unverified until supported by the fee notice or counterpart record. For the volunteer, write “zero active entries, one person present,” contact the organizer, and offer a place to wait or another way to get help.

College · Set the boundary and reconcile both sides

Start with three apples in storage and zero at the serving station. Record one transfer with a time and a shared ID. Reconcile storage and station separately, then reconcile the combined total. Compare the method to a transfer between two accounts and to an item filtered out of a public catalog.

Worked reconciliation

Storage: 3 − 1 = 2 apples. Station: 0 + 1 = 1 apple. Combined: 2 + 1 = 3 apples. The matching transfer ID ties the two entries to the same apple. For a catalog, record the public result and use the authorized archive to verify the item’s underlying status.

Graduate school · Account for transformation and uncertainty

A sample apple begins at 160 g. After processing, recover 126 g of slices, 20 g of core, and 8 g of juice. Define the system boundary and give the material balance. Design a follow-up that can distinguish material left on equipment from measurement error. If the question changes from grams to intact apples, report the new unit explicitly.

Worked balance

126 + 20 + 8 = 154 g recovered. The unresolved difference is 6 g. Recheck the scale and tare, weigh residue on tools and the cutting surface, and state the measurement uncertainty. The intact-apple count after slicing is zero for the processed apple. Its material is the subject of a separate balance.

Professional practice · Conductor capstone

Audit a fictional food drive. At 9 a.m., storage holds three apples and the serving station holds zero. Transfer ticket A-17 sends one apple from storage to the station at 9:15. At 9:30, two apples are observed in storage, three equal quarters are on serving plates, and a fourth is in reserve. The event account shows a $2 starting balance, a $1 fee, and a $1 ending balance. The fee recipient is absent from the statement. The roster shows one volunteer removed, and that volunteer arrives with a confirmation at 9:30. Create a one-page record another person can verify.

  1. Record each starting state, unit, source, and cutoff time.
  2. For each move, identify its origin, destination, amount, and supporting record. For division, account for every portion and any remainder.
  3. Report the expected and observed totals. Leave any remaining difference visible and give the next check.
  4. Record what assistance or follow-up the volunteer received. If a record needs correction, retain its original entry and link the dated correction.
Review the handoff

A-17 records two whole apples in storage and one apple transferred. At 9:30, two whole apples plus four quarters account for three apples’ worth of inventory, with two intact apples. The fee accounts for the $1 change in balance. Record its recipient as unverified and request the matching receipt or fee record. Record that the volunteer is present and ask the organizer to reconcile the roster and offer help while the entry is checked. A second reader should be able to trace each movement and identify what remains unresolved.
