# PART ONE — LOOK UP

## CHAPTER ONE — THE WEATHER BALLOON

Let me show you something so simple that everybody can look right at it and miss the point.

The National Weather Service sends up balloons with radiosondes. That is their word. A radiosonde is an instrument package. It measures temperature, humidity and pressure as it climbs. Its position can be tracked to calculate the wind. Its radio sends that information back down. A balloon you could see from the ground is carrying an instrument you might not recognize at all.

There is your first lesson.

THE BALLOON IS NOT THE WHOLE MACHINE.

And the machine is not explained by calling it a balloon.

The National Weather Service describes an ascent that can pass 115,000 feet. The gas expands as the pressure decreases. The balloon grows, eventually bursts, and the instrument comes down by parachute. Launches happen routinely around the world. Some people have seen them. Most people have lived their whole lives without thinking about them twice.

I am not interested in making an ordinary weather balloon into something it isn't. I am interested in refusing to make it less than what it is.

There is a gas envelope. There is a payload. There is radio equipment. There is a receiving station. There are measurements. There are people who operate the whole damn thing.

Now change the payload.

Same sky. Different job.

Now change the mission.

Same word on the outside. Different system underneath it.

Now stop telling me the label answers the question.

A person who wants to understand something does not stop at the name on the box. Open the box. Follow the wires. Follow the signal. Find the receiving station. Find the people who ordered it built. Follow the money that paid for it.

That is not magic. That is common sense.

And common sense is exactly where this book begins.

## CHAPTER TWO — THE BALLOONS THE MILITARY DIDN'T PUT ON THE WEATHER REPORT

Now that we have a weather balloon, let's keep the balloon and change the purpose.

PROJECT MOGUL.

The United States ran a classified high-altitude balloon program in the 1940s. The mission involved instruments intended to help detect Soviet nuclear activity. The government did not advertise every detail while the work was under way. Years later, its own reports discussed the program in connection with the government's explanation of debris found near Roswell, New Mexico, in 1947.

There is no need for me to invent that sentence. Their records put those words together.

CLASSIFIED.

BALLOON.

MILITARY.

INSTRUMENT.

NUCLEAR.

Put those five words on the same page, because the government already did.

It does not turn every balloon into a secret operation. It tells you something much more useful: the same general technology can be put to different purposes, including purposes the public is not told about at the time.

You can argue about somebody's theory all day long. You cannot make the actual historical existence of a classified balloon program disappear by laughing at the word *balloon*.

So I am not laughing. I am looking.

Look at the payload. Look at the altitude. Look at the receiver. Look at the year. Look at who issued the contract. Look at the name that appears on the government page, then read the page instead of letting somebody paraphrase it for you.

The point isn't that I know every operation hidden behind every name.

The point is that names are not operations.

A document can tell you what was ordered. A test record can tell you what was tried. A flight log can tell you what flew. A payment record can tell you what somebody bought. Those are different facts, and I want all of them standing on their own feet.

NOT ONE OF THEM HIDING BEHIND THE OTHER.

## CHAPTER THREE — THE GIANT BALL NASA PUT INTO ORBIT

Now here comes one of my favorite pieces of their own history.

NASA put a giant balloon into orbit.

Not a five-foot weather balloon with a radiosonde dangling below it.

A one-hundred-foot-diameter Mylar sphere coated to reflect radio signals. Echo 1A. Launched August 12, 1960.

ONE HUNDRED FEET.

ROUND.

REFLECTIVE.

ARTIFICIAL.

ORBITING EARTH.

NASA called the project Echo. NASA's own account described a giant metal balloon in orbit used to bounce signals from one part of the world to another. The receiving stations, their locations and the communications experiments appear in the technical records. The balloon was the reflector. Other equipment did the transmitting and receiving.

That means the whole system mattered.

The object up above. The signal sent from below. The station listening somewhere else. The geometry between them. The people who designed it. The money that built it.

Do you see why I keep saying attributes, characteristics and functionalities?

Because if I had told somebody, without giving the NASA date, that the United States put a giant shiny ball in the sky to bounce communications around the Earth, somebody would have called it a crazy story.

Then I open NASA's own history.

And there it is.

I do not need to dress it up. It is interesting enough with the numbers left exactly where they belong.

NASA's records call it a passive communications satellite. Engineers at Langley developed the balloon concept, Goddard managed the project, and communications work involved JPL and Bell Laboratories. Those names are part of the record too. I do not have to pretend one office did everybody's work.

But I am not going to let a few different organizational names make the whole space race disappear from the same period of history. Huntsville, the Army Ballistic Missile Agency, NASA, military rocketry and orbital communications were all part of a fast-moving technological world.

The dates connect.

The institutions connect.

The capabilities connect.

What they actually built must be established by what they actually built, not by what anybody wants the reader to imagine.

And that is where the next set of plans gets mighty interesting.

## CHAPTER FOUR — REDSTONE ARSENAL DIDN'T NEED HOLLYWOOD TO THINK ABOUT THE MOON

Let's go back to 1959.

Before anybody turned a lunar military base into something you could point to on a movie screen, the United States Army put one on paper.

PROJECT HORIZON.

A U.S. Army Study for the Establishment of a Lunar Military Outpost.

That is the real title of the June 8, 1959 study. The Army Ballistic Missile Agency conducted it. Wernher von Braun appointed Heinz-Hermann Koelle to lead the project work. It examined the supplies, rockets, operations, structures, crews and logistical requirements of putting a military outpost on the Moon.

It did not consist of two drunk fellows saying, 'Wouldn't it be cool if we built something up there?'

It contained actual engineering planning.

The Army's own retrospective describes seventy-five Saturn II rockets for initial construction material and equipment and sixty-four rockets a year for support. It describes nuclear power, living quarters, storage, laboratories, communications, recreation, a hospital and modules protected by lunar material.

In other words, the Army had already thought about the people, their food, their water, their power and where the hell they were supposed to sleep.

ABOVE AND BELOW.

A structure on the surface. A protected interior. A logistics chain running all the way back to Earth.

Now that is the kind of paper I want to read.

I have spoken about Redstone plans and budgets. A plan has a date and a name and a sheet and a purpose. A budget has numbers. An organization has people assigned to work. Those details matter more to me than a thousand polished explanations from somebody who hasn't opened the first folder.

The Army record says Project Horizon remained a feasibility study. Fine. Let that sentence stand in the record alongside all the extraordinary details of the study itself. I will not call a proposed base a completed base simply because I like the concept. And I will not make the actual proposal disappear simply because someone tells me it stayed on paper.

BOTH FACTS STAY.

The study existed.

The military purpose existed.

The engineering questions existed.

And Redstone was part of that history.

Anybody who claims there was no serious military planning for a lunar outpost has a date to look up now.

June 8, 1959.

## CHAPTER FIVE — THE PEOPLE UNDER THE GROUND

Now put the Moon on one side of your mind and the Earth on the other.

Do not mash them together. Look at the same engineering problem from both ends.

How do you keep people alive when the environment outside can kill them?

How do you protect communications?

How do you get water, air, power and supplies into a protected place?

How do you keep operating when something goes wrong above ground?

The government did not wait on a science-fiction movie to ask any of those questions.

GREENBRIER.

A resort in West Virginia. A public building with an extraordinary second purpose. During the Cold War, the U.S. government secretly built a relocation facility for Congress beneath the resort's expansion. People came and went without realizing what was below. The facility eventually became public knowledge in 1992.

That is their history, not something I dreamed up.

CAMP CENTURY.

The U.S. Army constructed a facility in Greenland beneath the ice. A place with tunnels, sleeping spaces, scientific work and power. The Army also considered a larger missile network called Project Iceworm. A built camp and a much bigger proposed system are two different entries in the ledger; both belong in the story of what the government was preparing for.

THULE.

An early-warning radar installation in Greenland. Part of a Cold War system designed to notice incoming threats and send information to decision-makers before time ran out.

Look at the world they were designing: places above, places below, places at the edge of the ice, places that talked to other places, and machines trying to see what human beings couldn't see with their own eyes.

That is not one secret world map I have to invent.

Those are real chapters of the Cold War with different dates and different functions.

Now ask what an engineer would compare when designing such systems.

Power. Communications. Protection. Redundancy. Supply. Distance. Time.

SAME QUESTIONS. DIFFERENT LOCATIONS.

The mistake is not comparing them. The mistake would be claiming two separate installations are the same installation without a document connecting them.

So keep the documents in the right folders, and keep reading.

## CHAPTER SIX — THE MOON ALARM THAT ACTUALLY REACHED THE MILITARY

Here is a remarkable sentence from the military record.

On October 5, 1960, radar echoes from the Moon appeared in the Thule warning system and were initially identified as a potential missile threat. The alarm was treated as false. Investigators traced it to reflections from the Moon, and procedures were changed to filter out the problem.

THE MOON SHOWED UP ON A MILITARY WARNING SYSTEM.

Not as a Hollywood special effect.

As a real radar return, in a real military system, at a real moment in history.

The declassified history of strategic warning records it. The date and the explanation are there.

Now think about the connection to Project Echo in that same year.

NASA was deliberately bouncing radio signals off an artificial reflective satellite. The military was using radar to watch for missiles, and a lunar reflection caused a false alarm.

Two systems. Different missions. The same physical phenomenon of signals traveling through space and reflecting from objects.

It matters that the objects and systems are not identical. It also matters that a person looking up at the sky would never know all of that from the appearance of a light overhead.

That is exactly why I keep asking for the underlying record.

What bounced?

What was transmitted?

What was received?

Who saw the return?

What conclusion did the operators draw?

What did the later investigation report?

Their documents answer specific questions. Those answers give us a way to test other questions without inventing the answers in advance.

And I am not finished asking.

# PART THREE — THEIR TRUTH, IN THEIR OWN RECORDS

## CHAPTER TEN — THE PEOPLE WHO CALLED THEMSELVES CHRISTIANS

Now I want to tell you why the words *their own records* mean so much to me.

Because I have watched institutions talk about righteousness while their paperwork told another story.

America loves calling itself a Christian nation.

Not the Sunday sermons.

THE LAWS. THE CHILDREN. THE SLAVEHOLDERS. THE PREACHERS. THE MONEY.

In 1662, Virginia's lawmakers enacted a rule tying a child's enslaved or free status to the condition of the mother. Think about what that actually meant to an enslaved woman and to her child. A white man could father a child with an enslaved woman, and the resulting child could be treated as enslaved under the law.

That wasn't a question about the child's humanity.

The child was human before the lawmakers touched a pen.

That was a question about POWER.

Who could control the mother? Who could control the child? Who benefited from a law that made human bondage descend through birth?

The legislators wrote their answer.

In 1667, Virginia wrote another law saying Christian baptism did not change a person's condition of bondage. A human being could be baptized and remain enslaved. The people who made the law weren't merely confused about what the church building was for. They specified the difference between religious conversion and civil liberty in law.

THEY COULD RECOGNIZE YOUR SOUL AND STILL SELL YOUR BODY.

Now move forward to 1807.

An edition of the Bible printed for enslaved people in the British Caribbean selected passages and removed much of the material about deliverance, including important portions of Exodus. The physical publication survives. It is an artifact you can inspect and compare.

I don't have to guess whether somebody edited scripture for a slave society.

The book is there.

Then 1845. A major Baptist institutional split over slaveholding and missionary appointments. Then 1995, when the Southern Baptist Convention adopted a resolution acknowledging its historical relationship to slavery and racism.

THE INSTITUTION SAID IT.

One hundred and fifty years after its founding, it acknowledged what its own history already showed.

## CHAPTER ELEVEN — THREE FIFTHS OF WHAT, AND FOR WHOSE BENEFIT?

Now look at the Constitution.

The original 1787 document counted enslaved people under a three-fifths apportionment rule when calculating representation. The people held as slaves did not get the freedom or the vote that should belong to human beings. But their numbers could increase the political power exercised by the states where they were enslaved.

Ask who benefited.

Not the enslaved child.

Not the mother whose family could be sold away.

Not the man forced to work without freedom.

Who gained political representation from the population of people they denied political rights?

There is your question.

The Constitution's formula was not a claim that a Black person was biologically three-fifths human. It was a political formula with consequences for actual human beings. Giving the accurate definition of the formula is the beginning of the examination, not an excuse for what it helped sustain.

1861. The Civil War begins. Mississippi's secession declaration names slavery as central to its reasons for leaving the Union.

1863. The Emancipation Proclamation takes effect in designated areas in rebellion, not every place in the country where an enslaved person was held.

1865. The Thirteenth Amendment is ratified. Slavery and involuntary servitude are prohibited except for the constitutional criminal-punishment exception.

1868. The Fourteenth Amendment changes the constitutional apportionment formula and guarantees citizenship and equal protection under its terms.

1870. The Fifteenth Amendment prohibits denial of voting rights on account of race, color or previous condition of servitude.

And between those dates and after them came continuing violence, coerced labor, discriminatory laws and struggles to make constitutional promises mean something in people's lives.

I don't want a list of amendments used as a shield between the reader and the people who suffered.

I want the dates because dates show when institutions changed their words.

I want the human history because it shows what happened before and after the words changed.

I want the original legal text because the people who made the system should not get to hide behind somebody else's paraphrase of it.

THEIR LAW.

THEIR POLITICAL POWER.

THEIR MONEY.

THE HUMAN COST.

Put them all on the same page and stop acting like one cancels the others.
