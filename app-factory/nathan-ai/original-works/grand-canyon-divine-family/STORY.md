# Divine-family Grand Canyon — complete concept sequence

**Authored interpretation attributed to Nathan Tabor.** Mythological creative work; not presented as historical fact.

## Version A — God's back porch
From his immense heavenly back porch, God looks upon Earth. A stream descends from heaven and strikes the earth below. The land grooves, furrows and channels as loose ground can do when water passes over it. Increase the imagined scale to a divine giant: the Grand Canyon is the story's enormous result.

## Version B — Baby Jesus
1. A playful young Jesus escapes the attention of God the Mother.
2. From a cloud high above Earth, the child releases a stream aimed at the landscape below. His back is turned to the viewer.
3. The stream reaches the land; branching channels grow and merge into a vast canyon landscape.
4. God the Mother finds the child and disciplines him.
5. God the Father, in jealous anger, condemns God the Mother to the underworld.
6. Her disappearance becomes the story's explanation for why a divine Mother is absent from later dominant theological accounts.
7. The child looks down at the canyon, unaware of the full family conflict he has triggered.

## Themes to preserve
Childhood observation; fluid mechanics; erosion; divine scale; laughter and mischief; motherhood; injustice; paternal jealousy; underworld exile; the contested history of feminine divinity; innocence and authority.

## Format possibilities
- Children’s picture book: focus on the child, the gigantic stream, canyon formation and clear gentle consequences. The severe exile story requires age-appropriate handling.
- Mythological longform: explore the complete Father–Mother–Son–Spirit cycle, exile and contested religious traditions.
- Critical edition: annotate each scientific/historical question separately from the fictional narrative.

## Invariants
Never portray any part of this fiction as a verified Biblical event, a historical explanation for church doctrine, or the geological origin of the Grand Canyon. Keep the author's original narrative distinct from editorial description.
