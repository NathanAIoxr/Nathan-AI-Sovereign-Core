PHYSICS & SECURITY

Learn the mechanics.

Change one variable at a time and see what happens to the bounce, shape and boundary response.

Super-restitution

For a stationary wall, the normal velocity after impact is modeled as v′ = −e · v. For e > 1, the bounce requires an energy input. The simulator supplies this energy and applies a speed limit and a containment ceiling.

Try it: Start at 0.75, then use 1.25. Set burst to zero to isolate restitution.

Soft-body deformation

A ring of points responds to a restoring spring force F = −k · Δx, neighbor coupling, damping and a shared pressure term. The ring squashes on impact and stretches as the points recover.

Try it: Compare stiffness 25 and 80 with the same launch.

Kinetic burst

Each ball starts with a finite simulated charge. A fast enough collision spends some of the charge to add outward velocity and release glowing sparks.

Try it: Set restitution to 1.00 and compare burst at 0% and 100%.
