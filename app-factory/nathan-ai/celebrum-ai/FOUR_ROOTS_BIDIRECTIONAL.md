## Inverse stress tests: what CELEBRUM exposes about the legal roots

1. **Power versus performance:** The same standard that bars a ruler from inventing authority bars software from inventing permission, claiming hidden access, or falsely reporting an external action.
2. **Liberty versus labeling:** A person asking “what if” is not automatically declaring a physical fact. An automated system should classify the request first, answer it as fiction, inquiry or research, and show the exact ground for any capability limit. A story about ants becoming gods is fictional; allowing the story doesn't establish the theology.
3. **Ownership versus appropriation:** An author's original word sequence is an immutable source record. Every copy, editorial expansion, translation or generated chapter is a distinct derived record with a link back to that source; legal ownership and licensing must be determined under applicable law, not presumed from database attribution.
4. **Harm versus deflection:** A child-safety report needs preservation, appropriate protective triage, privacy and lawful escalation. A model must not hide documented misconduct, equate accusation with conviction, or falsely declare an investigation completed. The 1,000 SNAKES is a symbolic witness-and-self-audit mechanism, not destructive software.

## Historical distinctions that must remain explicit

The seventeenth-century roots here are English/common-law foundations influencing subsequent colonial and U.S. law, **not** American federal statutes of the 1600s. Rights were uneven: chattel slavery, colonial dispossession, and exclusions existed alongside constitutional rhetoric. The Thirteenth and Fourteenth Amendments substantially changed the constitutional framework. This does not weaken the audit thesis; it gives the book two separate questions: **Which promises were broken? Which protections were absent from the promise itself?**

The Statute of Frauds applies to certain categories of agreements, not every promise. The castle doctrine had legal limits; the Habeas Corpus Act implemented procedural protections and did not guarantee that arbitrary imprisonment became impossible.
