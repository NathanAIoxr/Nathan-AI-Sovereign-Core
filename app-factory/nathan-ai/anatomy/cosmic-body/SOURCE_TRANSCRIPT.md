1. The 114-Chakra Yogic System

Classical yogic traditions describe 114 chakras formed where the body's 72,000 nadis (energy pathways) intersect.
• Internal and External Points. Out of the 114 junctions, 112 reside inside the physical body and 2 exist outside the physical form.
• Major, Minor, and Micro Centers. These are often categorized into the 7 major chakras along the spine, 21 minor chakras (located at joints, organs, palms, and soles), and 86 micro chakras spread throughout the body.
• Actionable Pathways. Yogic teachings note that 108 of these internal junctions can be actively worked with through meditation, breathwork (pranayama), and posture.

2. Higher and Lower Chakras (The 14-Chakra Model)

Certain traditional Hindu Shaiva texts, such as those preserved by the Himalayan Academy, map 14 primary chakras.
• Seven Higher Centers. These run from the base of the spine to the crown of the head and govern higher consciousness, intellect, love, and spiritual perception.
• Seven Lower Centers. Located below the base of the spine down through the legs to the soles of the feet (Atala through Patala), these govern instinctual survival states such as fear, anger, jealousy, and greed.

3. Early Tantric and Buddhist Systems

Before the seven-chakra model became the standard, different lineages used varying counts depending on the specific meditation practice.
• 5-Chakra Buddhist Systems. Early Indo-Tibetan Buddhist tantras (around the 8th century CE) typically focus on four or five main energy wheels located at the navel, heart, throat, and crown.
• 9, 12, and 21+ Tantric Maps. Texts like the Vijnana Bhairava Tantra and various Kaula Shaiva traditions work with systems of 9, 12, or more than 21 focal points, including centers at the palate (talu), forehead (lalata), and above the head (dvadashanta).
