Volume I · The A, B, and C Trinities

Three letter groups bring myth, vocabulary, and cultural history into the same reference. A pairs light and strategy with the weighing of the heart. B connects creation, the death of Baldr, and protection. C puts time beside an entrance to the Underworld and a guide who knows the route ahead.

A · Origin, Light, & Victory

Apollo

Greek & Roman mythology

Light, music, prophecy, and truth. Apollo came to be identified with the sun. The origin of his name is debated.

Athena

Greek mythology

Wisdom, strategic warfare, and crafts. A familiar account places her birth at the head of Zeus.

Anubis

Ancient Egyptian mythology

The guide of the dead attends the balance in the weighing of the heart. Ma’at supplies the standard of truth, and Osiris presides over judgment.

Alpha

Greek alphabet

The first Greek letter, derived from the Phoenician letter aleph, whose name means ox.

Ares

Greek mythology

The god of war, associated with the tumult of combat.

Atlas

Greek mythology

The Titan who bears the heavens. His name is used for the uppermost cervical vertebra and for collections of maps.

Avalon

Arthurian legend

In Geoffrey of Monmouth’s account, Arthur’s sword Caliburn was forged on Avalon, where the wounded Arthur was eventually taken.

B · Thunder, Wisdom, & Song

Brahma

Hindu tradition

The creator in the Trimurti alongside Vishnu and Shiva, associated with the Vedas and cosmic generation.

Baldr

Norse mythology

A radiant god, son of Odin and Frigg. His death precedes Ragnarök in the mythic sequence.

Bastet

Ancient Egyptian mythology

A protective goddess depicted in lioness and cat forms and associated with music and celebration.

Beth / Bayit

Hebrew & Phoenician

Beth is the name of the second letter. Hebrew bayit is the word for house or dwelling.

Brigid

Irish tradition

Traditions link the goddess Brigid and namesakes to poetry, healing, and smithcraft.

Boreas

Greek mythology

The personified north wind, associated with cold and winter weather.

Beowulf

Old English epic

The Geatish hero battles Grendel, Grendel’s mother, and a dragon.

C · Time, Hunt, & Underworld

Chronos / Cronus

Greek mythology

Chronos personifies time. Cronus is the Titan of the Golden Age. The names and figures overlap in some later traditions.

Cerberus

Greek mythology

The hound who guards the entrance to the Greek Underworld, often depicted with three heads.

Circe

Greek mythology

The sorceress of Aeaea transforms Odysseus’s crew, restores them, and gives Odysseus directions for his onward journey.

Cernunnos

Ancient Celtic religion

An antlered figure attested in Gaulish iconography. Broader associations with wild animals and natural cycles are interpretations.

Chimera

Greek mythology & biology

A fire-breathing creature combining lion, goat, and serpent. In biology the term describes an organism with cell populations from genetically distinct sources.

Camelot

Arthurian legend

The legendary location of Arthur’s court in medieval romance.

Cosmos

Greek kosmos

Order, arrangement, or ornament. The word came to designate an ordered universe.

Volume II · The Trinity of T’s and the V Shift

the trinity of t's - theseus (the Zeus), theos (the OS), and thanos (thank NOS - nitrous oxide) by Nathan Tabor

Theseus brings Athenian civic order. Theos brings the word “gods” into the conversation through John 10:34–35 and Psalm 82. Thanos moves from a Marvel character to the language of engine performance. The V chapter brings together word, truth, life, and voice.

T · The Trinity of T’s

Theseus

Greek mythology

The legendary Athenian king and labyrinth traveler. Nathan Tabor’s sound reading is “the Zeus.”

Theos

Biblical Greek

A Greek word rendered as God or god by context. John 10:34–35 quotes Psalm 82:6 and uses plural forms for “gods.” Nathan Tabor’s sound reading is “the OS.”

Thanos

Marvel fiction

Marvel’s Titan uses the Infinity Stones to erase half the population. Nathan Tabor’s sound reading is “thank NOS - nitrous oxide.”

Taw

Semitic scripts

A historical ancestor of Greek tau and Latin T. The Hebrew word taw means a mark in Ezekiel 9:4 (KJV).

Tiwaz / Tyr / Tiw

Germanic & Norse traditions

A name family associated with the Germanic deity Týr and oaths. “The Was” is the sound reading explored in the source notes.

Thoth / Tehuti

Ancient Egyptian mythology

A deity of writing, reckoning, and learned arts. “Thought” is the sound reading explored in the source notes.

Tengri

Turkic & Mongolic traditions

A term associated with heaven, the sky, and the deity in steppe traditions.

Tonatiuh

Nahuatl / Mexica tradition

A solar figure associated with the Fifth Sun.

Tlaloc

Nahuatl / Mexica tradition

A deity of rain, storms, and fertility.

Tiamat

Akkadian / Mesopotamian tradition

The primordial sea in Enūma Eliš. Marduk divides her body to form the cosmos.

Tao

Classical Chinese

The Way or path, romanized Tao or Dao.

Tesla

Physics

The SI unit of magnetic flux density, named for Nikola Tesla.

Torque

Mechanics

The moment of a force about a point or axis.

Thor

Norse mythology

The thunder figure cross-referenced from B.

Taw / Tau

Semitic & Greek alphabets

A crossed mark is an early form associated with the letter that became Greek tau.

Titan

Greek mythology

A member of the divine generation preceding the Olympians. The proposed sun and stretch roots are uncertain.

Tithēmi

Greek vocabulary

To place, put, or establish.

V · Word, Truth, & Life

Verbum

Latin

Word. The Latin Vulgate uses Verbum for Logos in John 1:1.

Veritas

Latin

Truth or truthfulness.

Vita

Latin

Life.

Vox

Latin

Voice, utterance, or sound.

Varuna

Vedic Sanskrit

A deity connected with moral and cosmic order.

Vayu

Vedic Sanskrit

Wind and the deity of wind.

Vav / Waw

Hebrew & Phoenician alphabets

A letter name associated with a hook. The prefixed letter can express “and.” Its numeric value is six.

Vulcan

Roman mythology

The Roman deity associated with fire and the forge.

Vector

Mathematics & physics

A quantity represented by magnitude and direction in elementary geometry and physics.

Volt

Physics

The SI unit of electric potential difference, named after Alessandro Volta.

Volume III · Earth to Fate · G through L

The letter groups run from Gaia, Ganesha, and Gabriel to Loki, Lakshmi, and Lugh. The notes trace common threads such as messengers, thresholds, kingship, and the signs behind several Greek and Hebrew letters.

G · Earth, Wisdom, & Guardianship

Gaia

Greek mythology

The personified Earth. In Hesiod she bears Uranus, the starry sky, and Pontus, the sea.

Ganesha

Hindu tradition

The elephant-headed deity associated with wisdom, beginnings, and removing obstacles.

Gabriel

Biblical and cultural traditions

A messenger in Daniel and Luke. The Hebrew name is built from the G-B-R family and El. See the Hebrew name notes.

Gimel / Gamma

Hebrew & Greek alphabets

Greek gamma follows Phoenician gimel. Camel and throwing stick are proposed readings for the early sign.

Gilgamesh

Mesopotamian epic

The king of Uruk seeks immortality after his companion Enkidu dies.

Gorgon

Greek mythology

The sisters Stheno, Euryale, and Medusa, associated with a petrifying gaze. Medusa was mortal.

Grail

Arthurian legend

The vessel at the center of a quest in Arthurian romance. Its nature and significance vary by story.

H · Sky, Hearth, & Strength

Hera

Greek mythology

Queen of the Olympians and a goddess of marriage, associated with Zeus on Olympus.

Hermes

Greek mythology

Messenger, protector of travelers and boundaries, patron of commerce, and guide of the dead.

Horus

Ancient Egyptian mythology

A falcon deity associated with the sky and kingship. Some traditions link his eyes with the sun and moon.

Hestia

Greek mythology

Goddess of the hearth and its sacred fire. The Homeric Hymn links her with first and last libations at banquets.

Heracles / Hercules

Greek & Roman mythology

The hero famed for his strength and Twelve Labors. Hercules is the familiar Roman name.

Hades

Greek mythology

Ruler of the Underworld, where Persephone is queen. The name also came to refer to the realm.

Het / Eta

Hebrew & Greek alphabets

Greek eta comes through Phoenician heth, whose early sign is often read as a fence or enclosure.

I · Magic, Rainbow, & Fire

Isis

Ancient Egyptian mythology

A major goddess of magic, healing, and motherhood, associated with restoring Osiris and protecting Horus.

Indra

Vedic tradition

A deity of storms and thunder who wields the vajra in the Vedic hymns.

Iris

Greek mythology

Personification of the rainbow and a messenger of the gods.

Icarus

Greek mythology

The son of Daedalus who falls after flying too close to the sun on wings fastened with wax.

Ishtar / Inanna

Mesopotamian mythology

Akkadian Ishtar and Sumerian Inanna are closely identified goddesses associated with love, war, and power.

Izanagi & Izanami

Japanese mythology

The creator pair who stir the waters with a jeweled spear in accounts of the islands’ formation.

Iota / Yod

Greek & Hebrew alphabets

Greek iota follows Phoenician yod, with an early sign associated with a hand or arm.

J · Sky, Thresholds, & Sovereignty

Jupiter / Jove

Roman mythology

Roman deity of the sky and thunder. Jupiter and Zeus belong to the inherited Indo-European sky-god name family.

Janus

Roman mythology

God of beginnings, doorways, and transitions, often depicted with two faces. January bears his name.

Juno

Roman mythology

Queen of the Roman gods, associated with marriage and civic protection. She is conventionally equated with Greek Hera.

Jormungandr

Norse mythology

The World Serpent encircling Midgard, who confronts Thor at Ragnarök. Also written Jörmungandr.

Jade Emperor / Yu Huang

Chinese traditions

A celestial sovereign in Daoist and Chinese popular religion, frequently portrayed presiding over a divine bureaucracy.

Jotun

Norse mythology

A member of the jötnar, beings who appear in kinship and conflict with Norse gods.

J

Latin alphabet

Developed as a written variant of I. Its use as a separate consonant letter became established gradually.

K · Time, Action, & Kingship

Kali

Hindu tradition

A fierce goddess associated with time, transformation, and destruction. Her roles vary across devotional traditions.

Krishna

Hindu tradition

A major deity, often identified as an avatar of Vishnu, who teaches Arjuna in the Bhagavad Gita.

Kronos

Greek mythology

Another romanization of Cronus, the Titan overthrown by Zeus. Compare Chronos / Cronus under C.

Kaph / Kappa

Hebrew & Greek alphabets

Greek kappa follows Phoenician kaph, a letter name associated with the palm of a hand.

Kitsune

Japanese folklore

The Japanese word for fox. Fox spirits in folklore are often depicted with shapeshifting abilities and sometimes multiple tails.

Khonsu

Ancient Egyptian mythology

A lunar deity whose name is often interpreted in connection with traveling.

Kukulkan

Maya tradition

A Maya feathered serpent figure especially associated with Yucatán. Compare Quetzalcoatl under Q.

L · Light, Trickery, & Fate

Loki

Norse mythology

The shapeshifting trickster who helps and harms the gods in different stories. The association with fire is an interpretation.

Lakshmi

Hindu tradition

Goddess of prosperity, fortune, beauty, and abundance.

Lugh

Irish mythology

Known for mastery of many arts and associated with the festival Lughnasadh. His name’s relation to light is debated.

Lamed / Lambda

Hebrew & Greek alphabets

Greek lambda comes through Phoenician lamed. An ox goad is the usual interpretation of the early sign.

Logos

Greek philosophy & biblical Greek

A word with senses including word, speech, account, or reason. Its philosophical and scriptural meanings depend on the passage.

Leto

Greek mythology

The Titaness who bears Apollo and Artemis.

Leviathan

Biblical & ancient Near Eastern traditions

A sea creature described in Job 41 and elsewhere in the KJV, with comparisons to other ancient Near Eastern sea-monster traditions.

Volume IV · Water to Order · M through R

These six letter groups run from Mercury, Minerva, and Morpheus to Ra, Rhea, and Ṛta. Along the way, the lexicon connects mythic themes to vocabulary for breath, measure, order, and speech.

M · Water, Memory, & Measure

Mercury

Roman mythology

God of trade and a messenger. His name is commonly linked to the Latin family for merchandise or commerce.

Minerva

Roman mythology

Goddess of crafts, intellect, and strategy, commonly compared to Athena. The name’s deeper derivation is debated.

Morpheus

Classical mythology

In Ovid, a maker of dream figures who imitates human forms. Greek morphē means form or shape.

Mem / Mu

Hebrew & Greek alphabets

Greek mu follows Phoenician mem, whose early sign is conventionally associated with water.

Ma'at

Ancient Egyptian religion

Truth, justice, and right order, also personified as a goddess. Compare the heart-weighing scene under Anubis.

Mars

Roman mythology

God of war and a protector invoked in agricultural rites. The origin of the name is uncertain.

Maya

Sanskrit

Māyā has senses such as art, power, or illusion in different texts. The proposed measure association belongs to the source interpretation.

N · Night, Victory, & Abyss

Nyx

Greek mythology

The primordial personification of night.

Nike

Greek mythology

The winged personification of victory. The Greek noun means victory.

Neptune

Roman mythology

God of waters, later closely identified with Poseidon. Moisture-related name roots are proposed.

Nun / Nu

Hebrew & Greek alphabets

Greek nu follows Phoenician nun. Fish is a traditional interpretation of the letter name, with other proposals for the early sign.

Nemesis

Greek mythology

A personification of retribution or due allotment. Her name is associated with the Greek family for distributing or apportioning.

Nergal

Mesopotamian mythology

A deity associated with war, disease, and the Underworld. Scorching heat is one association.

Nirvana

Sanskrit

Literally associated with extinguishing or blowing out. In Buddhist contexts the term concerns liberation and the cessation of suffering.

O · Vision, Horizon, & Circle

Odin

Norse mythology

One-eyed seeker of knowledge and runes. His name family is associated with inspiration, excitement, or frenzy.

Osiris

Ancient Egyptian mythology

God of the dead and regeneration, associated with agriculture and continuity after death. His name’s derivation is uncertain.

Orion

Greek mythology

The giant hunter placed among the stars in Greek myth. The constellation has long served as a seasonal marker.

Ayin / Omicron

Hebrew & Greek alphabets

Phoenician ayin, associated with an eye, supplied the ancestral sign for Greek omicron.

Ouroboros

Greek terminology & ancient iconography

A serpent eating its own tail. The Greek term names the tail-devouring image.

Oceanus

Greek mythology

The Titan and world-encircling river in archaic Greek cosmology.

Oracle

Latin oraculum

A prophetic answer, the medium that delivers one, or the place of consultation. The Latin word family includes speaking or praying.

P · Sea, Spring, & Hearth

Poseidon

Greek mythology

God of the sea, earthquakes, and horses. Interpretations of his name as lord or husband of earth are proposals.

Persephone

Greek mythology

Queen of the Underworld. Her return from below ground is linked to seasonal fertility. The supplied name roots are conjectural.

Prometheus

Greek mythology

The Titan who brings fire to humanity. His name is traditionally understood as forethought.

Pe / Pi

Hebrew & Greek alphabets

Greek pi follows Phoenician pe, whose name is associated with the mouth.

Pan

Greek mythology

A deity of shepherds, flocks, and wild places, and the namesake of panic. His proposed name derivation is uncertain.

Pneuma

Greek vocabulary

Wind, breath, or spirit depending on context.

Pluto

Greek & Roman mythology

A name for the ruler of the Underworld, associated with wealth and resources from beneath the ground.

Q · Quetzal, Quirinus, & Quintessence

Quetzalcoatl

Nahuatl & Mesoamerican traditions

The Nahuatl name combines quetzal plumage with serpent. This feathered serpent is associated with wind and creation in several traditions.

Quirinus

Roman mythology

An early Roman deity later associated with the deified Romulus. Proposed name explanations include a civic assembly and a spear.

Quintessence

Latin quinta essentia

Fifth essence. In ancient and medieval natural philosophy it names the fifth element distinct from earth, water, air, and fire.

Qoph / Koppa

Hebrew & archaic Greek alphabets

Greek koppa follows Phoenician qoph. Head and needle-eye readings are among the proposals for the early sign.

Quantum

Latin and physics

Latin quantum asks how much. Modern physics uses quantum for a discrete quantity in a particular system.

Quake

Old English and modern English

To shake or tremble, from Old English cwacian.

Quest

Latin and modern English

A search or pursuit. The word traces through French to the Latin family of quaerere, to seek.

R · Sun, Flow, & Order

Ra

Ancient Egyptian mythology

A solar deity whose name is the Egyptian word for sun.

Rhea

Greek mythology

The Titaness and mother of a generation of Olympians. Flow and earth are proposed readings of her name.

Rta

Vedic Sanskrit

Ṛta concerns right order in Vedic texts, encompassing ritual and cosmic regularity.

Resh / Rho

Hebrew & Greek alphabets

Greek rho follows Phoenician resh, whose early sign is associated with the head.

Ruach

Hebrew vocabulary

Wind, breath, or spirit. The sense depends on context.

Romulus

Roman legend

The legendary founder of Rome and brother of Remus. Historical explanations of the name are uncertain.

Runes

Germanic writing

Characters of several early Germanic alphabets. The word family is associated with a secret or counsel.

Set / Seth

Saturn

Shin / Sigma

Hebrew & Greek alphabets

Shiva

Hindu tradition

A major deity whose name is commonly glossed auspicious. Traditions connect him with dissolution and renewal.

Sophia

Greek vocabulary

Wisdom, skill, or expertise, depending on the source and context.

Sovereign

English via Old French

A ruler or holder of supreme authority. The word family traces to a form meaning above.

Hebrew name notes

Each proposed reading is paired with a lexical or textual note.

Gabriel

Gavri'el · גַּבְרִיאֵל

My Strong Man is Might

G-B-R · strength, prevailing, or man in different forms. El is a Northwest Semitic word for deity. Daniel 8:15–16 describes an appearance like a man and names Gabriel. The proposed physical reading is an interpretation of the compound.

Michael

Mikha'el · מִיכָאֵל

Who is Like Power?

Mi is who, kə is like, and El is the divine element. The conventional gloss is Who is like God?

Raphael

Refa'el · רְפָאֵל

Power Mends / Stitches Together.

R-P-ʾ is the heal or restore word family. El is the divine element. The stitching image is a proposed physical reading.

Daniel

Daniyy'el · דָּנִיֵּאל

Power Judges / Executes Verdict.

The name belongs to the D-Y-N family of judging or vindicating. A conventional gloss is God is my judge.

Ezekiel

Yechezq'el · יְחֶזְקֵאל

Power Fastens / Grips Tight.

Ḥ-Z-Q is the be strong or strengthen family. The name is conventionally connected with God strengthening.

Uriel

Uri'el · אוּרִיאֵל

My Flame / Light is Power.

The light or flame family and El supply the elements of the name. God is my light is a conventional rendering.

Israel

Yisra'el · יִשְׂרָאֵל

Wrestles / Contends with Power

Genesis 32:28 (KJV) associates the name with prevailing. The historical grammar and exact sense of the name have multiple interpretations.

Samuel

Shemu'el · שְׁמוּאֵל

Name of Power

The name has been linked with the word for name and with the story of asking in 1 Samuel 1 (KJV). The exact historical derivation is uncertain.

Elijah

Eliyyahu · אֵלִיָּהוּ

My Power is Yah (Self-Existent Breath).

The conventional reading is My God is Yahweh. Yah or Yahu is a form of the divine name. The breath reading is interpretive.

Emmanuel

Immanu'el · עִמָּנוּאֵל

With Us is Power.

ʿImmanu means with us and El is the divine element. Matthew 1:23 gives the KJV rendering God with us.
