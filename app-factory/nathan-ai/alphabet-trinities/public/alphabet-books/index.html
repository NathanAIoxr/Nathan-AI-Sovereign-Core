III

Volume I · The A, B, and C Trinities

Three letter groups bring myth, vocabulary, and cultural history into the same reading index. A pairs light and strategy with the weighing of the heart. B connects creation, the death of Baldr, and protection. C puts time beside an entrance to the Underworld and a guide who knows the route ahead.

A · Origin, Light, & Victory

Apollo

Greek & Roman mythology

Light, music, prophecy, and truth. Apollo came to be identified with the sun. The origin of his name is debated.

Athena

Greek mythology

Wisdom, strategic warfare, and crafts. A familiar account places her birth at the head of Zeus.

Anubis

Ancient Egyptian mythology

The guide of the dead attends the balance in the weighing of the heart. Ma’at supplies the standard of truth, and Osiris presides over judgment.

Alpha

Greek alphabet

The first Greek letter, derived from the Phoenician letter aleph, whose name means ox.

Ares

Greek mythology

The god of war, associated with the tumult of combat.

Atlas

Greek mythology

The Titan who bears the heavens. His name is used for the uppermost cervical vertebra and for collections of maps.

Avalon

Arthurian legend

In Geoffrey of Monmouth’s account, Arthur’s sword Caliburn was forged on Avalon, where the wounded Arthur was eventually taken.

B · Thunder, Wisdom, & Song

Brahma

Hindu tradition

The creator in the Trimurti alongside Vishnu and Shiva, associated with the Vedas and cosmic generation.

Baldr

Norse mythology

A radiant god, son of Odin and Frigg. His death precedes Ragnarök in the mythic sequence.

Bastet

Ancient Egyptian mythology

A protective goddess depicted in lioness and cat forms and associated with music and celebration.

Beth / Bayit

Hebrew & Phoenician

Beth is the name of the second letter. Hebrew bayit is the word for house or dwelling.

Brigid

Irish tradition

Traditions link the goddess Brigid and namesakes to poetry, healing, and smithcraft.

Boreas

Greek mythology

The personified north wind, associated with cold and winter weather.

Beowulf

Old English epic

The Geatish hero battles Grendel, Grendel’s mother, and a dragon.

C · Time, Hunt, & Underworld

Chronos / Cronus

Greek mythology

Chronos personifies time. Cronus is the Titan of the Golden Age. The names and figures overlap in some later traditions.

Cerberus

Greek mythology

The hound who guards the entrance to the Greek Underworld, often depicted with three heads.

Circe

Greek mythology

The sorceress of Aeaea transforms Odysseus’s crew, restores them, and gives Odysseus directions for his onward journey.

Cernunnos

Ancient Celtic religion

An antlered figure attested in Gaulish iconography. Broader associations with wild animals and natural cycles are interpretations.

Chimera

Greek mythology & biology

A fire-breathing creature combining lion, goat, and serpent. In biology the term describes an organism with cell populations from genetically distinct sources.

Camelot

Arthurian legend

The legendary location of Arthur’s court in medieval romance.

Cosmos

Greek kosmos

Order, arrangement, or ornament. The word came to designate an ordered universe.

Volume II · The Trinity of T’s and the V Shift

the trinity of t's - theseus (the Zeus), theos (the OS), and thanos (thank NOS - nitrous oxide) by Nathan Tabor

Read the names aloud. Each sound reading connects a different domain to the lexicon. Theseus is the figure of Athenian civic order. Theos brings the word “gods” into the conversation through John 10:34–35 and Psalm 82. Thanos moves from a Marvel character to the language of engine performance.

In the letter history, taw and tau belong to the line of T. The line of V runs through a different development associated with waw and upsilon. The V chapter uses words such as Verbum, Veritas, Vita, and Vox to explore word, truth, life, and voice.

T · The Trinity of T’s

Theseus

Greek mythology

The legendary Athenian king and labyrinth traveler. Nathan Tabor’s sound reading is “the Zeus.”

Theos

Biblical Greek

A Greek word rendered as God or god by context. John 10:34–35 quotes Psalm 82:6 and uses plural forms for “gods.” Nathan Tabor’s sound reading is “the OS.”

Thanos

Marvel fiction

Marvel’s Titan uses the Infinity Stones to erase half the population. Nathan Tabor’s sound reading is “thank NOS - nitrous oxide.”

Taw

Semitic scripts

A historical ancestor of Greek tau and Latin T. The Hebrew word taw means a mark in Ezekiel 9:4 (KJV).

Tiwaz / Tyr / Tiw

Germanic & Norse traditions

A name family associated with the Germanic deity Týr and oaths. “The Was” is the sound reading explored in the source notes.

Thoth / Tehuti

Ancient Egyptian mythology

A deity of writing, reckoning, and learned arts. “Thought” is the sound reading explored in the source notes.

Tengri

Turkic & Mongolic traditions

A term associated with heaven, the sky, and the deity in steppe traditions.

Tonatiuh

Nahuatl / Mexica tradition

A solar figure associated with the Fifth Sun.

Tlaloc

Nahuatl / Mexica tradition

A deity of rain, storms, and fertility.

Tiamat

Akkadian / Mesopotamian tradition

The primordial sea in Enūma Eliš. Marduk divides her body to form the cosmos.

Tao

Classical Chinese

The Way or path, romanized Tao or Dao.

Tesla

Physics

The SI unit of magnetic flux density, named for Nikola Tesla.

Torque

Mechanics

The moment of a force about a point or axis.

Thor

Norse mythology

The thunder figure cross-referenced from B.

V · The V Shift

Verbum

Latin

Word. The Latin Vulgate uses Verbum for Logos in John 1:1.

Veritas

Latin

Truth or truthfulness.

Vita

Latin

Life.

Vox

Latin

Voice, utterance, or sound.

Varuna

Vedic Sanskrit

A deity connected with moral and cosmic order.

Vayu

Vedic Sanskrit

Wind and the deity of wind.

Vav

Hebrew alphabet

A letter name associated with a hook. The prefixed letter can express “and.” Its numeric value is six.

Vulcan

Roman mythology

The Roman deity associated with fire and the forge.

Vector

Mathematics & physics

A quantity represented by magnitude and direction in elementary geometry and physics.

Volt

Physics

The SI unit of electric potential difference, named after Alessandro Volta.
