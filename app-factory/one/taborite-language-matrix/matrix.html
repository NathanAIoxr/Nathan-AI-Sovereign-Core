THE TABORITE MODEL

Ogham Coder · Decoder · Cipher · Decipher · Universal Language Matrix — by Nathan Tabor. Ancient source preserved; additional multilingual, phonetic, and interpretive layers added alongside it.

Core invariant: 4 primary families × 5 stroke counts = 20 primary Ogham signs. Preserve the source text; add interpretation as a separate layer. 1 + 1 = 2 · 1 − 1 = 0.

1 · Working coder & decoder

Historical mode converts Latin letter equivalents to the 20 classical Ogham characters. It is a character transliteration, not a translation into Primitive Irish. Unsupported characters are preserved.

Universal mode: each Unicode scalar value is represented by exactly five classical Ogham signs interpreted as base-20 digits, with opening/closing markers. It can recover the exact characters of any valid Unicode text, including combining marks, RTL scripts and emoji. This is a new Taborite codec, not a historical Ogham spelling system or a language translator. The output is not secure encryption.

2 · Structural decoder — read the marks

Choose the side or angle relative to the stemline and the number of scores. This recovers the historical letter value from the geometry.

3 · The complete original 20-sign matrix

The rows are historical character groups. The names are traditional; converting every name into an original tree or spiritual meaning would be inaccurate.

5 · Evidence and limits

Historical facts: Ogham was used to write an early form of Irish; primary monumental inscriptions are mostly late antique and early medieval, especially on stone edges. Text normally rises from bottom to top. Medieval manuscript Ogham and its letter-name interpretations form a partly later tradition.

Tree-name caution: names such as Beithe (birch), Fern (alder), Dair (oak), and Coll (hazel) are defensibly botanical. Muin can mean “neck,” Gort “field,” and the origins of some names are disputed. Modern spiritual keyword lists are additional interpretations, not literal translations of what a stone inscription says.

Language caution: Unicode encoding is lossless conversion of text characters, not semantic translation. Undeciphered scripts and inscriptions cannot be magically translated by changing their glyphs.

6 · Historical Ogham ciphers and Nordic parallels

The medieval In Lebor Ogaim (The Book of Ogam), preserved in the fourteenth-century Book of Ballymote, recorded numerous deliberate Ogham cipher alphabets. Its Ogam Lochlannach (“Scandinavian Ogham”) and Gallogam (“foreigner Ogham”) connect the manuscript tradition to Younger Futhark runic material. That is documentary evidence of medieval comparative writing traditions, not proof that Ogham descended from Nordic runes.

7 · Complete 25-sign historical master chart + Peith

Original 4 × 5 grid remains intact above. The later forfeda are recorded separately. Historical values and spellings vary between sources and periods. Unlike the original twenty, the later signs cannot simply be read as a fifth family of 1–5 identical strokes.

Unicode supplement: ᚚ (Peith, U+169A) is also encoded, in addition to the traditional set of five extra letters. The Unicode names for ᚕ and ᚖ are Eabhadh and Or; the alternate labels “Koad” and “Óir” from the supplied comparison belong to interpretive or variant naming traditions, not their official Unicode names.

8 · Universal writing observatory

Inspect the original input from Section 1 without changing or simplifying its characters. Analyze Unicode code points, grapheme clusters, and script families. Every scalar can be given five exact base-20 Ogham digits; this is an encoding coordinate, not an ancient translation.

Select “Analyze current input” to inspect your text.

A displayed glyph can contain multiple code points (for example, accented letters or emoji sequences). The converter encodes scalar values exactly; the observatory additionally groups user-perceived characters using Intl.Segmenter when available. Script detection is approximate for shared punctuation and inherited combining marks.

9 · Spoken-language / phonetic laboratory

The International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA) describes human speech by sound features such as place, manner, voicing, vowel height and backness. These categories are a separate phonetic layer; Ogham’s stroke directions do not directly encode every human speech sound.

Microphone recognition is optional and browser-dependent. It may require network services; no voice data is recorded or stored by this page.

IPA recognition here is a selected feature dictionary, not a universal acoustic analysis engine.

To encode spoken words exactly, first create a transcript in orthographic text or IPA, then select universal Unicode mode in Section 1. Speech recognition availability and accuracy depend on the browser, language, privacy settings, and installed services. Ancient or unwritten languages require separately documented transcriptions.

10 · Taborite word and evidence ledger

Keep an authentic source separate from a researched meaning and your additional symbolic interpretation. Select an Ogham word or English spelling in Section 1 to see the letter-name associations. These poetic descriptions are provided as interpretive readings, not guaranteed meanings of ancient inscriptions.

The original Ogham alphabet itself does not supply word definitions. Its letter names can support a separately labeled poetic analysis.

An undeciphered script, a script that lacks Unicode characters, and an extinct language without attested pronunciation cannot automatically be translated or reconstructed with this tool. Its source text can still be preserved and studied if available in a usable form.
