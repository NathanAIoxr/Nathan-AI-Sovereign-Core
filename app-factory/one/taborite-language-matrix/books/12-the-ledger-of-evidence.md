# Volume 12 — The Ledger of Evidence

**The Taborite Universal Language Matrix — by Nathan Tabor**

*Research methods for avoiding false translations and preserving disputes*

> First-edition short manuscript. Research and interpretation are separated; see the series notes.

## Chapter 1: Five Columns

Use five columns for every claim: the artifact or utterance, exact source transcription, structural analysis, attested interpretation, and modern symbolic reading. Add citations and a date for each entry. A single image may support the geometry while telling us nothing about pronunciation.

When the original is damaged, record every ambiguous mark. When the meaning is disputed, store the competing hypotheses with their sources. Do not convert a missing source into a definitive negative claim.

The ledger is designed to resist both careless skepticism and careless certainty. What can be verified stays verifiable; creative insight remains available as creative insight.

## Chapter 2: The Disagreement Protocol

Two researchers may read the same stone differently. Rather than replace one record, attach a new reading with an explanation. Identify which marks cause disagreement. Date the revision and retain the earlier text.

A robust program can export the record as plain JSON alongside its human-readable explanation. This makes the result inspectable by another person or another piece of software. It also supports the user's request for an additive development policy.

No amount of software complexity eliminates uncertainty from a damaged source. The honest system can represent that uncertainty clearly.

## Chapter 3: The Question That Remains

Ask of each interpretation: Is it supported by the object itself, by a contemporary document, by a later tradition, or only by our present analogy? The four possibilities can all be recorded, but they must not be silently interchanged.

The Taborite reader is strongest when the application tells the reader which kind of claim it is making. That opens the model to criticism, growth, and future discoveries.

Preservation is not stagnation. It is what allows an original source and a new idea to coexist without either pretending to be the other.
