# Volume 11 — The Language Game of ONE

**The Taborite Universal Language Matrix — by Nathan Tabor**

*Learning design, accessibility, and a library of progressively harder exercises*

> First-edition short manuscript. Research and interpretation are separated; see the series notes.

## Chapter 1: Build and Clear

The project's arithmetic anchors are 1 + 1 = 2 for building and 1 − 1 = 0 for clearing. In a learning game, they can become simple operations: add one correct sign to a word, or clear an incorrect choice without deleting the original lesson.

The first challenges teach recognition. The second challenges ask players to reverse a description into a sign. The third asks them to read a short modern transliteration. The final challenges require them to identify when evidence is insufficient and to say so.

A learning machine should not reward confident invention. It should reward a correct boundary between known and unknown.

## Chapter 2: No Faces Required

A text-and-line interface can carry the entire experience. A vertical spine, moving strokes, letter glyphs, keyboard shortcuts, and accessible labels are enough to create a visual world. The car-lot tube-man comparison becomes a cheerful metaphor, not an invented historical character.

Give users high-contrast controls, clear error messages, and options to reduce motion. Store progress locally with a clear reset. The app should work without requiring login or payment.

The educational game ought to be playable by a child, but the scholarly notes should remain available to an adult researcher.

## Chapter 3: The Story of Mastery

Let each completed group unlock a new family of signs. A later level brings in script comparisons, and another introduces the interpretive three-plane model. The player can return to earlier lessons at any time.

The learning sequence is not a test of ancestry, religion, or hidden identity. It is a test of what one can actually read, encode, and explain. Victory means understanding the difference.

The final screen invites the player to write a short research note: What was the source sign? What did the program compute? What conclusion remains unproven?
