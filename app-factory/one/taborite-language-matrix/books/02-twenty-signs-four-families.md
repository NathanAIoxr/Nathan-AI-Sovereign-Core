## Chapter 1: Four Directions

The twenty primary characters of Ogham form four families of five. Each family is distinguished by the orientation of its strokes relative to the stemline. Within a family, the count increases from one to five. Written as a simple coordinate, a letter is a pair: family number and score number.

The standard sequence is B L F S N; H D T C Q; M G NG Z R; A O U E I. These are scholarly sound-value labels for the traditional signs, not a universal inventory of Irish pronunciation. The precise language represented in an inscription, and the history of its spelling, still matter.

The key mathematical property is not that every language has five strokes. It is that a compact coordinate system can distinguish a finite alphabet. The same idea can be used to design a new cipher without pretending that the cipher is ancient.

## Chapter 2: The Correct Coordinates

Use the second family for D: ᚇ has two strokes in Aicme hÚatha, not one stroke in a third family. Use the third family for M: ᚋ has one diagonal score in Aicme Muine. The fourth family contains the vowels. These details matter because one misplaced character changes a decipherment.

In a classroom exercise, draw a single straight stemline, choose a family, and then place from one through five strokes. Repeat for every family. The resulting table will display twenty positions. A word is then a string of coordinates, not a collection of arbitrary doodles.

For a modern name, record that the result is transliteration. An Ogham spelling of TABOR using modern Latin-letter equivalents does not transform the name into a historically documented Primitive Irish word.

## Chapter 3: A Checkable Decoder

A good machine-readable alphabet entry stores glyph, Latin equivalent, traditional name, family, stroke count, orientation, and notes about disputed readings. Keep the entire source token even when a particular screen cannot display the glyph. Never substitute an invented letter without flagging it.

A decoder built from this data can answer a narrow question exactly: given a known character, what alphabet position does it occupy? It cannot infer the meaning of an unknown language from stroke count alone.

This rule gives the matrix its honesty and its power. Deterministic operations have deterministic results; historical interpretation stays open to new evidence.
