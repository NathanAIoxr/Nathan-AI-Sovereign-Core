# Volume 4 — The Five After the Twenty

**The Taborite Universal Language Matrix — by Nathan Tabor**

*Forfeda, variant values, and the danger of forcing later material into the original grid*

> First-edition short manuscript. Research and interpretation are separated; see the series notes.

## Chapter 1: An Expanded Alphabet

Medieval scholars extended Ogham beyond its primary twenty signs. The later forfeda are associated with varied manuscript traditions and additional sound values. A modern Unicode chart provides characters for these signs, but a Unicode code point is not itself a historical pronunciation guide.

The common five are represented today as ᚕ ᚖ ᚗ ᚘ ᚙ. Their names and readings vary across sources and periods. Another supplementary character, ᚚ, is separately encoded. Preserve the distinction between the historical manuscript record and the modern digital standard.

One should not pretend that the forfeda form a straightforward fifth family with one through five identical strokes. Their shapes are more varied. Their history is a separate chapter in the development of the script.

## Chapter 2: The Name and the Sound

A novice may see a convenient chart assigning EA, OI, UI, IO, and AE to the five additions. Such values can be a useful teaching shorthand, but it is incomplete as a claim about every historical manuscript. A reliable digital tool should state which reference system it follows.

An implementation can support several labeled tables and let the reader switch between them. Each table needs a documented source, date, and editorial policy. The original input must remain unchanged when the display table changes.

This is additive research. A new view should never erase the older view or quietly convert a variation into an absolute rule.

## Chapter 3: The Expansion Test

Give the program a line of Ogham that uses one supplementary symbol. Ask it first to identify the exact Unicode character. Then ask which reading table is being applied. Only then generate the Latin-letter equivalent. If two tables disagree, return both possibilities with references.

A fabricated universal decoder tends to hide uncertainty. A trustworthy one exposes it. The extension of the twenty-sign matrix is therefore an exercise in source fidelity as much as in mathematics.
