# Volume 10 — The Three Architectural Planes

**The Taborite Universal Language Matrix — by Nathan Tabor**

*Below, Middle, and Above as an explicitly interpretive reading of position*

> First-edition short manuscript. Research and interpretation are separated; see the series notes.

## Chapter 1: Below: Foundation

The Taborite Model associates the area beneath the viewer with foundation, underworld, and subconscious. A floor mosaic can be read through those themes, but the subject and use of a particular ancient floor must be checked independently.

In the source record, describe the actual position: floor, threshold, crypt, step, or buried fragment. Record what the mosaic depicts and where it was found. Then record the user's Below association as a separate interpretation.

This order keeps the archaeological evidence available to everyone, including readers who do not share the symbolic framework.

## Chapter 2: Middle: Encounter

A wall mosaic may meet a viewer at eye level. In the Taborite Model, that position belongs to the conscious, social, and lived world. The idea creates an accessible way to study physical placement and the viewer's bodily experience.

Not every wall image was originally installed at modern eye level. Some have been moved, restored, or reconstructed. The original building program may be partially lost. Therefore, place and orientation are historical questions before they are metaphors.

Middle is also the place in this model where a scholar and artist may meet: the first describes what is supported, and the second composes what the evidence inspires.

## Chapter 3: Above: Looking Up

Domes, ceilings, upper walls, and vaults ask a viewer to lift the eyes. The Taborite Model associates that posture with the sky, heavens, cosmos, and supra-consciousness. These are proposed conceptual meanings, not universal labels assigned by every ancient culture.

A Byzantine gold mosaic may carry a documented sacred subject. Another ceiling may portray a ruler, geometry, astronomy, or decoration. The interpretation becomes stronger when it addresses the actual image instead of erasing it.

Below, Middle, and Above together form an imaginative reading instrument. Its reliability depends on keeping physical description, historical explanation, and the new symbolic reading distinct.
