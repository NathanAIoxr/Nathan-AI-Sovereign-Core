## 2. Primary-source findings: Josephus, Papyrus 115, Antiochus, charagma

### Josephus: the 6 CE Judean assessment and the Fourth Philosophy
Josephus, *Antiquities* XVIII.1, says Quirinius was sent to assess properties after Judea came under Roman administration. Judas, associated with Gamala, and Saddok denounced this taxation as an introduction to slavery and called for liberty. Josephus later characterizes Judas’s Fourth Philosophy as believing God alone is their ruler or lord; he also condemns the violent political aftermath he associates with the movement. This is historically relevant to sovereignty and resistance, but Josephus did **not** describe an ancient digital exit strategy. Sources: [S1].

### The census and Luke 2 should not be silently equated
Luke 2 associates Jesus’s birth story with Quirinius while other birth chronology places it in Herod the Great’s lifetime. Scholarship notes a dating tension: Herod died around 4 BCE, whereas Quirinius’s Judean census is generally dated around 6 CE. This is a debated historical/textual issue, not solved by renaming the census an absolute tracking mechanism. Sources: [S7], [S8].

### Revelation 13:18, 666 and 616
Papyrus 115 (P.Oxy. 4499) supports a **616** reading. Other ancient witnesses support **666**, and 616 is known from Christian discussion already in the second century. A well-known gematria analysis maps *Neron Caesar* in Hebrew letters to 666 and *Nero Caesar* without the final nun to 616. This is a strong scholarly interpretation, not an exclusive mathematical proof. Sources: [S3], [S4], [S9].

### Antiochus IV and mandatory conformity
1 Maccabees 1:41–61 reports serious religious repression, inspectors, forced sacrifices and harsh punishments. The claim of an across-the-board retail market token system, let alone the world’s first commercial boycott, is not demonstrated by that passage. Source: [S2]. For the user’s KJV-only reading practice, label 1 Maccabees clearly as a historical text rather than silently presenting it as one of the sixty-six canonical KJV books.

### Charagma
Greek `χάραγμα` (Strong’s G5480) can mean a mark, stamp or carved image. It does not by itself establish that every buyer in the Roman Empire was required to burn incense and obtain a trading token. Source: [S6].

### Theological and metaphorical layer
Isaiah 45:7 (KJV), Job 1–2, Revelation 13, and 2 Corinthians 4:4 are legitimate textual anchors to study, but an identity equation *God = Satan*, or a universal modern equation *every phone transaction = Caesar worship*, remains interpretation rather than an independently established textual or historical fact. Preserve the original expression verbatim in the source layer; make interpretive labels explicit in the research layer.
