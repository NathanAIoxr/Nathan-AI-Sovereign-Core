THE BOOK

CHAPTER 1 — DISNEY HAS PINOCCHIO. NATHAN TABOR HAS ASSOCCHIO.

Disney has PINOCCHIO. Nathan Tabor and the Bible have ASSOCCHIO. The lie is the engine of the satire: PINOCCHIO'S NOSE GROWS. ASSOCCHIO'S FRONT ASS HANGS OVER HIS BELT.

ASSOCCHIO is a fictional IFB-preacher caricature built to challenge the gap between what a preacher PREACHES, what a preacher PRACTICES and what a child is TAUGHT TO ACCEPT. This visual gag is the author's satire, not a physical claim about real ministers.

CHAPTER 2 — A GRAND LIFE. ONE DROP OF WATER.

Luke 16:19–31 (KJV) tells of a rich man living in luxury while Lazarus suffers at his gate. After their deaths the rich man, now suffering, begs for water. The author's parable comparison is between earthly comfort and the consequences of refusing to help someone in need.

Revelation 21:8 (KJV) names “all liars” in its warning about final judgment. The rich-man narrative does not itself say wealth proves lying; ASSOCCHIO combines the passages as the author's moral satire.

CHAPTER 3 — THE FIRST TIME I HEARD THE N-WORD USED DISRESPECTFULLY

AUTHOR'S FIRSTHAND ACCOUNT Nathan Tabor reports that the first time he heard the N-word used disrespectfully was by IFB preacher Bobby Robertson at Gospel Light Baptist Church in Walkertown, North Carolina.

He reports that instead of rebuking the preacher, his parents subsequently sent him and his siblings to school there. The exact words attributed to the preacher have not been supplied, and this account is presented as the author's recollection, not an independently corroborated finding.

CHAPTER 4 — THE PARENTS. THE SCHOOL. THE INDOCTRINATION.

“Instead of my parents rebuking him, they put us in school there.” The author's argument is about the choices of adults who selected a child's church and school. The institution they trusted became responsible for the education of children who had not selected it themselves.

THE PREACHER. THE PARENTS. THE INDOCTRINATION. Three steps in the author's chronology, kept as testimony rather than recast as institutional proof.

CHAPTER 5 — WHEN YOU LAY DOWN WITH DOGS, YOU GET UP WITH FLEAS.

“When you lay down with dogs, you get up with fleas I’ve never laid down with those people, but my family did”

In this proverb, the author distinguishes the family's association from his own consent. He says he was raised inside an institution he did not independently choose. The saying is a metaphor about human choices, not a judgment about actual dogs.

CHAPTER 6 — THE TRANSFER. THE SILENCE. THE CHILDREN.

DOCUMENTED REPORTING The Fort Worth Star-Telegram's December 2018 investigation found at least 412 allegations of sexual misconduct involving 187 IFB churches and affiliated institutions. It described 168 leaders accused or convicted of sexual crimes against children and at least 45 accused people continuing in ministry after allegations came to light. Reporting documented cases of church-to-church relocation and institutional failures to protect children.

AUTHOR'S ALLEGATION — NOT A VERIFIED COUNT Nathan Tabor has alleged that more than 400 offending preachers were moved without any arrests, using church funds. The 2018 tally is 412 allegations, not 400 transferred preachers. The assertion that none were arrested is contradicted by reported arrests. Specific claims about funds require financial records. Those distinctions are preserved here rather than silently changing the author's allegation into a fact.

CHAPTER 7 — THE CHRISTIAN NATION OF AMERICA

“The Christian Nation of America is the only oxymoron more accurate than the oxymoron of military intelligence”

The author's satire tests religious national language against institutional conduct: a label cannot substitute for the treatment of people, especially children and those asking for help. The question is whether professed beliefs result in protection, accountability and care.

CHAPTER 8 — THE BODY OF CHRIST IS SITTING WAITING ON JESUS TO RETURN

The author's question is about immediate responsibility: What happens when people wait for someone else to rescue the vulnerable while harm continues?

Luke 10:37 (KJV) ends the Good Samaritan account with “Go, and do thou likewise.” In the author's framing, protection requires action: take a child seriously, protect immediate safety, preserve evidence, report suspected crimes to civil authorities and do not substitute transfers for investigation.

CHAPTER 9 — THE BOOK IMAGE

The requested educational image concept: the proverb WHEN YOU LAY DOWN WITH DOGS, YOU GET UP WITH FLEAS; a school and church labeled Gospel Light Baptist Church; a symbolic adult choice to associate with a religious institution; a child's lack of control over that choice; and “N-word” as the abbreviated term in the author's testimony. A fictional ASSOCCHIO character can represent hypocrisy, but should not be presented as a verified portrait of Bobby Robertson.
