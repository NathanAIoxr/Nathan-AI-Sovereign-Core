## AI foundations — technical interpretation

The owner's three pillars are retained as the organizing brief: mathematical foundations; programming languages and execution; frameworks. Their universal wording is qualified below.

### 1. Mathematics
Linear algebra, calculus, probability, statistics, optimization, mathematical logic and discrete mathematics support different AI approaches. Neural networks commonly encode inputs as tensors and use matrix multiplications alongside nonlinearities, normalization and other operations. Not every AI algorithm is a neural network: decision trees, symbolic rules and search have different structures.

Backpropagation uses the chain rule to compute gradients of a loss with respect to parameters. An optimizer such as gradient descent or Adam uses those gradients to update parameters. Loss is not always an “error rate”; training does not guarantee correctness, convergence or generalization. Inference ordinarily uses trained parameters without retraining on each answer.

### 2. Languages and hardware
Python is a widely used development interface, not a universal AI language or requirement. Expensive operations often run in compiled CPU or accelerator kernels. C/C++, CUDA, compiler-generated kernels and hardware-specific libraries can participate; Python is not merely a wrapper in every system. Deployments can use JavaScript, Swift, Java, Rust or other languages and runtimes.

### 3. Frameworks
PyTorch and TensorFlow provide tensor operations, automatic differentiation and model-building tools. JAX and scikit-learn are additional examples; custom and symbolic systems need neither of the first two. The “HTML of AI” phrase is an analogy, not a technical equivalence.

### 5. Baphomet — the later occult symbol

Baphomet has a different history from the ancient biblical Satan.

The familiar goat-headed, winged image was developed by Éliphas Lévi in the nineteenth century, combining symbols of opposing forces, balance and esoteric philosophy. It was subsequently adopted or reinterpreted by occult and Satanist movements.

It is not an ancient surviving portrait of Satan or a recognized member of the original Hebrew divine council.
