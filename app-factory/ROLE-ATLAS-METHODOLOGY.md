# HISTORICAL FORENSIC ROLE ATLAS
## SYS-ARCH-ROLE-MAP-002 — source and function methodology
Version: 2026-10-09

**Question:** Who was called a hero, savior, messiah, chosen one, liberator, villain, or adversary? What did the actor actually do? Did the root problem end?

This is an expandable cross-cultural research index, **not** an exhaustive list of every character in world history, nor a definitive ranking of moral worth. The dataset currently contains **135 indexed cases**. Entries without attached sources are **research candidates, not verified historical findings**. Even sourced entries need additional corroboration before final publication.

## Fundamental distinction
- **Honorific/claim:** How followers, rivals, institutions, legends or later audiences describe the person or force.
- **Operational role:** Rescue, defense, repair, punishment, reform, revolution, teaching, healing, rebuilding or exploitation.
- **Mechanism:** Specific acts, instruments, institutions and constraints.
- **Outcome:** Measured or textual consequences, including duration and affected population.
- **Root cause:** Whether the institution, incentive, exploit, pathogen or harmful mechanism is gone, reduced, redesigned, or unchanged.
- **Aftermath:** Whether protection is durable and people can live freely without a continuously renewed antagonist.

Do not conclude that every acclaimed hero did nothing because universal evil was not eradicated. A verified rescue and an incomplete structural reform can both be real accomplishments. Do not conclude that the complete annihilation of an adversary automatically destroys society: the "Undying Villain Rule" is a **creative hypothesis**, not an empirical law. Examples include global eradication of naturally circulating smallpox in 1980 and rinderpest in 2011.

## Eight primary functional classes
1. **Protector / guardian / champion / hero** — defends people or places against identified harm, sometimes only temporarily.
2. **Rescuer / deliverer / savior** — gets people out of immediate danger; may not correct conditions causing danger.
3. **Liberator / abolitionist / reformer** — changes coercive institutions, legal regimes or structures; assess how far reforms extend.
4. **Founder / architect / transformer / revolutionary** — creates new institutions or political systems; new systems may carry new harms.
5. **Teacher / prophet / healer / guide / bodhisattva** — offers teaching, care or salvation guidance. These are *functionally overlapping but religiously distinct*.
6. **Messiah / Christ / Saoshyant / Mahdi / avatar / future Buddha** — terms with distinct languages, theology, scriptures and expectations. **Do not present as exact synonyms or settled identities.** Compare promised functions.
7. **Chosen one / anointed / destined actor** — denotes authorization, prophecy or narrative assignment; does not itself prove action or result.
8. **Antagonist / opponent / adversary / villain / oppressor / perpetrator** — not equivalent categories. An antagonist may merely oppose a protagonist. A perpetrator is defined by documented harmful acts. Natural predators, entropy, black holes, disease and antimatter have no moral agency and should not be called morally evil in a factual science report.

Other cross-cutting roles: witness, survivor, beneficiary, enabler, bystander, mediator, trickster, teacher, companion, collective, institutional actor, and root-cause mechanism.

## Six outcome tests
- **R0 — Title only:** Reputation, prophecy or public praise without a demonstrable outcome.
- **R1 — Immediate rescue:** A specified person or group is protected or extracted.
- **R2 — Localized victory:** A specific threat or perpetrator is stopped.
- **R3 — Reform:** A law, institution, practice or source of harm is changed.
- **R4 — Durable root repair:** Reproduction mechanism of the specific harm is disabled and safeguards are sustained over time.
- **R5 — Verified eradication:** The defined target no longer occurs naturally; substantiated by coordinated surveillance and scientific or documentary evidence.
- **RF — Fictional / theological completion:** Resolution occurs *within a text or tradition*, not as a demonstrated historical event.
- **RU — Unresolved / unknown:** Insufficient information, continuing conflict or conflicting interpretations.

R-levels are **not** scores for the value of human lives or of religious beliefs. Many people make substantial contributions without producing R5 outcomes. Compare accomplishments against the *actual, bounded objective*, not an artificially infinite goal.

## Narrative implementation
When building a story using the Story Blueprint:
1. Assign roles without assuming that every opposition is evil.
2. Write a one-sentence **promise made** and an observable **success condition**.
3. Distinguish personal villain from the machinery enabling harm.
4. Show direct action and corroborating consequences; do not grant victory for rhetoric alone.
5. Permit lasting victory: a narrative can move forward through discovery, kinship, exploration, restoration and creation without reviving the identical evil.
6. Apply **R0–R5 / RF / RU** to the *outcome of a specific action*, not to human dignity.
7. Offer both recurring-conflict and root-elimination narratives, without requiring a villain for every sequel.
