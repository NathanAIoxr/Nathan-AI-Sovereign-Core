## Imported source scope

The supplied 12-section reference includes principal deities, primordials, Titans and descendants, underworld places and spirits, nature divinities, ensembles, human experiences personified, nymph classes, creatures, legendary mortals and treasures. The material has been transformed into **316 individually addressable reference records** (some names intentionally appear more than once in different categories, so these are not 316 unique individuals). Six ensemble definitions preserve the original grouping distinctions.

**Primary source preservation:** Original prose remains in the originating chat; `atlas.json` is an attributed *normalized secondary representation* with concise descriptions and source-status fields. Independent research and primary classical references per claim are still required. Do not overwrite the original by treating this parsed index as a verbatim copy or published historical encyclopedia.

## Important cross-category rules

- **Olympians:** The canonical Twelve is a council of twelve, not fourteen simultaneous council seats. Hestia/Dionysus vary; Hades is a major deity but normally not in that council.
- **Elder Titans:** Twelve specific Gaia/Uranus offspring; Eurybia is not one of those twelve.
- **Chronos ≠ Cronus:** Primordial Time in Orphic tradition is distinct from Titan Cronus, despite later conflations.
- **Underworld ≠ Christian Hell:** Greek accounts of Hades, Elysium, Asphodel and Tartarus vary and do not imply the later theological framework.
- **Greek *daimones*** can mean personifications and spirits, not simply Christian evil demons.
- Duplicate labels such as `Dike`, `Eirene`, `Lampades`, or `Cerberus` across contexts retain independent **record IDs**, with any eventual *same-as* links subject to editorial review.
- Names like `Thalia (Muse)` and `Thalia (Grace)` remain separate. Heroes and equipment are not all gods.
- Alternative genealogies and cult traditions must be retained instead of forcing a single canonical tree.
