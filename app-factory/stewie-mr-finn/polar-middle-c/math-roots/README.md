# THE MIDDLE C — 100 Mathematical Roots

A playable, offline-first browser branch for Nathan Tabor's Stewart & Mr. Finn / THE MIDDLE C mathematics game.

## Included
- 100 numbered practice gates across 11 eras, each with a scored formula-matching challenge.
- Head = Above C (guess), Gut = Middle C (equation test), Soles = Below C (inputs, sources), limbs = coordinates and operations.
- Six real numerical methods: bisection, false position, Newton, secant, fixed-point and bracketed hybrid. Five selectable functions, iteration trace, graph and explicit failure states.
- Two-variable Newton demonstration with a calculated Jacobian and residual. Sampled sign-change scan is not a proof of finding all roots.
- Offline iPhone WKWebView copies in Resources/PolarMiddleC/math-roots.
- Child-inclusive free-first experience. No registration, ads, analytics, payments or server calls.

## Critical historical distinctions
The user's timeline is a creative working reference, not independent peer-reviewed proof of chronology. In the pasted Markdown, many original expressions were lost. The equations here are **modern learning reconstructions**, not transcriptions from ancient artifacts.

The groups numbered **1–5, 13–20 and 56–65** were broad categories, not 23 individual verified ancient formulas. These numbered gates explicitly use modern practice variants. Source attributions and era boundaries require additional historical research; in particular, the Chinese remainder theorem has different sources than the Nine Chapters, and "Gaussian elimination" is a modern term for related historical methods.

Anchors independently checked: NYU Institute for the Study of the Ancient World on Babylonian YBC 7289 (https://isaw.nyu.edu/exhibitions/before-pythagoras/items/ybc-7289/); CUNY/LibreTexts on Egyptian circle approximation (https://math.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Geometry/Elementary_College_Geometry_%28Africk%29/07%3A_Regular_Polygons_and_Circles/7.06%3A_Area_of_a_Circle).

Mathematical roots, linguistic etymological roots and the creative C = consciousnesses metaphor are clearly different. They must not be treated as interchangeable evidence.
