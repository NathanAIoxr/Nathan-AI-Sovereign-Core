THE FIELD MANUAL

Closer to the center. Farther from safety.

Invaders get nearer the Citadel even as their supply cost rises. Defenders trade time, barriers, and information for survival.

01 — Roles and winning

Vanguard begins at chamber 01 with 32 Proviant and must reach chamber 63 with positive supply. Citadel begins with 15 Fortification and wins by holding through Round 12 or reducing enemy Proviant to zero.

02 — Tactical actions

Each faction begins its turn with 3 Tactical Action Points (TAP). The Vanguard can march 3 or 7 chambers, recover +7 Proviant once per round while at chamber 43 or earlier, scout decoys, or breach a gate immediately ahead. Every action costs 1 TAP. A long march costs more Proviant than a short march, with added surcharges starting at chambers 22 and 44.

The Citadel can apply pressure (2 enemy supply damage; costs 2 Fortification), raise gates at chokepoints (12, 24, 36, 48, 60), plant one decoy, or rebuild +4 Fortification once per round. A gate costs 2 TAP and 3 Fortification; other actions cost 1 TAP.

03 — The 32 Enigmas

Both factions receive 3 hidden lore cards. Playing a card costs 1 TAP and triggers the effect for the active faction. Used cards cycle back when the deck empties. There is no random dice roll in combat resolution; randomized deck order uses a fixed seed for reproducibility.

04 — Fog and false signals

Decoys are fictional board hazards. Their location is hidden from Vanguard on the board, but scouting removes them; walking through one costs 3 supply. A defensive gate physically stops a march and must be breached. Overt announcements, ambiguous tokens, and counter-intelligence are contained entirely inside the game.

05 — Modes and limits

Solo pits Vanguard against a simple scripted Citadel opponent. Pass-and-play allows two humans on one device. This version is a proof of the economy, geometry, and card system; it does not yet implement a tactical line-of-sight battle scene or online multiplayer.

06 — Source discipline

The 32 original enigma labels contain a mix of established reference points and highly specific historical claims that have not been independently verified here. They are treated as source-preserving fictionalized gameplay lore, not a validated reference encyclopedia.

Mirror principle

The Nathan AI creative assistant would faithfully reflect the author’s intended meanings by default. Reversal, opposition, or Devil’s Advocate modes should be expressly selected by the user, never silently imposed.

Safety and player agency

Optional player-chosen tactics can customize challenge. No automatic IQ ranking, personal psychometric diagnosis, targeting of real-life vulnerabilities, off-game deception, or tracking of private communications is required.
