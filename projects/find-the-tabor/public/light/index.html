  1. Documented

    Hebrew Bible / KJV · Tabor is a named place

    Judges 4, Psalm 89:12, Jeremiah 46:18 and Hosea 5:1 name Tabor in their own contexts. These are the baseline texts for testing an earlier wording or place tradition.

    • KJV · Judges 4, Psalm 89:12, Jeremiah 46:18, Hosea 5:1
    • Hebrew and Aramaic Lexicon of the Old Testament · תבור
  2. Documented

    First-century Gospel accounts · unnamed mountain

    Matthew 17:1–9, Mark 9:2–8 and Luke 9:28–36 recount the Transfiguration. In the KJV, Matthew explicitly describes a shining face and bright clothing. All three leave the mountain unnamed. The KJV is a 1611 translation.

    • KJV · Matthew 17:1–9, Mark 9:2–8, Luke 9:28–36
  3. Documented

    KJV · Light, testimony and false appearance

    John 8:12 supplies an early Christian light-of-the-world formulation independent of a Tabor place-name. John 3:21 concerns coming to the light, 2 Corinthians 4:4 the glorious gospel of Christ, and 2 Corinthians 11:14 the angel-of-light image. Compare the phrases in their own contexts.

    • KJV · John 8:12, John 3:21, 2 Corinthians 4:4, 11:14
    • KJV · Matthew 17:1–9, Mark 9:2–8, Luke 9:28–36
  4. Documented

    325 and 381 · creedal light language

    Both transmitted creeds use Light of Light in their description of Christ. These lines concern the Son’s relationship to the Father. Neither creed identifies or renames Mount Tabor. This is distinct from the later Roman feast.

    • First Council of Nicaea · 325 CE creed
    • First Council of Constantinople · 381 CE creed
    • Vatican News · 1457 inclusion in the Roman calendar
  5. Documented

    Mid-fourth century · Cyril names Mount Tabor

    In Catechetical Lecture 12 §16 Cyril of Jerusalem explicitly places the Transfiguration at Mount Tabor. This gives a dated post-Gospel witness to the place tradition. Earlier attributions such as Origen need the exact text and edition verified.

    • Cyril of Jerusalem · Catechetical Lecture 12 §16
  6. Documented

    404 CE · Jerome records Paula’s ascent

    Letter 108 describes Paula climbing Mount Tabor and associates it with the Transfiguration. Keep the 404 letter separate from Jerome’s earlier Gospel revision when tracing the transmission of wording.

    • Jerome · Letter 108, §13, 404 CE
  7. Documented

    Fourteenth century · an argument over the nature of the light

    Gregory Palamas treats the light of Tabor as divine and uncreated. The 1341–1351 Constantinopolitan controversy involved different accounts of the light’s nature. The relevant decisions belong to Byzantine church history.

    • Gregory Palamas · Homily on the Transfiguration, English translation
    • Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam · Dissertation section on Palamas and divine light
  8. Documented

    1457 · the Roman liturgical calendar

    Vatican News dates Pope Callixtus III’s inclusion of the feast in the Roman calendar to 1457 in connection with the victory at Belgrade the previous year. It also names Mount Tabor. This is a concrete change in Roman liturgical practice.

    • Vatican News · 1457 inclusion in the Roman calendar
  9. Documented

    2002–2011 · Catholic use of both the place and Christ-centered framing

    John Paul II in 2002 and Benedict XVI in March 2006 explicitly name Tabor and identify the event with Christ. Benedict’s August 2006 and March 2011 statements emphasize the light as divine glory or God’s light. Compare the published texts for co-use and differences in emphasis.

    • John Paul II · Angelus, 24 February 2002
    • Benedict XVI · Angelus, 12 March 2006
    • Benedict XVI · Angelus, 6 August 2006
    • Benedict XVI · Angelus, 20 March 2011
  10. Author framework

    “Tabor Light being rotated to fake light aka Christ Light”

    Nathan Tabor’s thesis asks who shifted the label, when, in which text, and with what effect. The dated record supports changes in wording and liturgical framing. It contains explicit co-use of Tabor and Christ language in Catholic sources. No primary document located in this pass establishes a deliberate Vatican renaming or the appropriation of an earlier non-Christian doctrine called Tabor Light.
