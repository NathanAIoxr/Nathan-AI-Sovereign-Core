A single PC tower hits thermal, power, and memory walls immediately, so an AI system needs thousands of custom matrix-math chips wired together with light.
Here is the exact hardware manifest for that architecture, built from the silicon up.

1. The Compute Engine (Custom Tensor Silicon)

Standard CPUs process instructions sequentially, and standard gaming GPUs waste die space on 3D graphics rendering. For pure AI math, every square millimeter of silicon goes toward matrix multiplication and high-speed memory bandwidth.
• Core Processors — Google Ironwood (7th-Gen) or 8th-Gen TPU Pods: Instead of a single chip, I would deploy a 9,216-chip Ironwood TPU Pod. Each liquid-cooled chip features dual compute dies delivering 4,614 TFLOPs of FP8 performance and 192GB of HBM3e high-bandwidth memory. Linking all 9,216 chips creates 42.5 Exaflops of compute and 1.77 Petabytes of shared addressable memory.
• Host Control Processors — Google Axion (Armv9) CPUs: Every rack of Tensor chips needs a host processor to manage operating systems, data pipelines, and network traffic. Custom Axion processors (built on Arm Neoverse V2/N3 cores) handle orchestration while using up to 60% less energy than traditional x86 server CPUs.
• Hardware Security & Offload — Titanium Microcontrollers: Dedicated microcontrollers sit between the processors, storage, and network to handle encryption, lifecycle management, and I/O routing without stealing compute cycles from the main processors.

2. Interconnects & Optical Switching

Moving trillions of parameters between chips is the biggest bottleneck in AI. Copper wires lose signal integrity and generate massive heat over distance, so the machine has to communicate using light.
• 3D Torus Inter-Chip Interconnect (ICI): Within each pod, every TPU connects directly to its nearest neighbors in three dimensions. Six bidirectional ICI links give each chip 1.2 TB/s of direct chip-to-chip bandwidth without passing through an external network switch.
• MEMS Optical Circuit Switches (OCS): Instead of static copper cables, the racks connect through mirrors controlled by Micro-Electro-Mechanical Systems (MEMS). These microscopic mirrors physically bounce laser beams between fiber-optic ports, allowing the computer to dynamically rewire its own physical topology in milliseconds to route around hardware failures or reshape itself for different workloads.
• Jupiter Data Center Fabric: At the building scale, a 13+ Petabit-per-second software-defined network links multiple pods together so hundreds of thousands of chips act as a single logical computer.

3. Memory, Storage & Cooling Infrastructure

At exascale speeds, traditional spinning hard drives and air fans cannot keep up.
• Tiered Memory Hierarchy: Active model weights live directly inside the 1.77 PB HBM3e stacks on the chips, while context caches and active datasets sit in multi-terabyte DDR5 host RAM pools.
• Distributed NVMe Flash Storage (Colossus): Massive arrays of enterprise NVMe solid-state drives act as a unified, self-healing global file system, streaming training checkpoints and petabytes of multimodal data over 400Gbps optical links.
• Direct-to-Chip Closed-Loop Liquid Cooling: Air cannot pull heat away from 9,216 densely packed chips fast enough. Cold plates circulate chilled liquid directly across the silicon dies, carrying heat out to facility cooling towers so the processors never throttle their clock speeds.

4. Software & Orchestration Layer

Hardware without a unified compiler leaves most of that silicon sitting idle.
• Pathways Orchestration System: Rather than locking one program to one server, Pathways schedules tasks across thousands of chips simultaneously, splitting massive neural networks across multiple pods seamlessly.
• OpenXLA & JAX Compiler Stack: Translates high-level mathematical graphs directly into low-level machine instructions tailored to the exact physical layout of the TensorCores and SparseCores.
