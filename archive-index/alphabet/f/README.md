## Letter F, cut-open P, Nordic and Celtic scripts

**Author geometry:** F can be constructed by opening P's closed upper bowl and making the upper and middle bars straight. The normalized capital F illustration has three visible straight strokes. The owner did not assign an original stick count for F or P, so none is invented.

**Documented historical scripts (not a common derivation):**

- Latin **F** traces through ancient Greek **Ϝ** (digamma) and Phoenician *waw*. It is **not historically derived from Latin P** on the evidence currently cited.
- Germanic/Nordic runic **ᚠ** represents **f**, reconstructed as Elder *Fehu* and historically named Old Norse *fé* (cattle/wealth). Elder Futhark had 24 characters; later Viking-Age Younger Futhark had 16.
- **ᛈ** is a separate older rune conventionally transliterated **p**, associated with *Perthro / Peorð*. The exact ancient rune-name etymology is uncertain; its shape does not prove origin of P or F.
- Irish **Ogham ᚃ** (*Fearn/Fern*) represents F in the first five-sign series B L F S N. It is **not a Nordic rune**. In the Taborite app its three scores lie on the correct side of the stemline, different from Latin F's three visible segments.
- The earlier author's **stick-figure stretching** comparison with forms such as ᛉ and ᛗ remains a **creator visual analogy**, separate from historical rune sound values, names and manuscript evidence.
- F, P, FF, PH, V and voiced/voiceless /f/ comparisons should be studied with pronunciation and language metadata, not auto-normalized as one historical word.
