## Core design: what "D" opens

The letter `D` opens separate connections, each with source labels and reversible wordplay, rather than forcing one interpretation:

- **Dungeons:** Confined or obstructed choices in a life narrative; identify the decision, source, and consequence.
- **Dragons:** Biblical imagery studied in context, including KJV Revelation 12:9; do not silently equate a literary monster, a human being or a scientific animal.
- **Deliverance:** Resolve a challenge through a recorded ethical decision, a verified passage, or a new understanding.
- **David:** KJV 1 Samuel 17. The original David vs. Goliath works keep their separate source identities.
- **Daniel:** KJV Daniel 6. Study character, conscience, government and outcome from the text itself.
- **Discernment:** Compare a claim, the cited passage and the player's explanation without inventing biblical quotations.
- **Duty:** James 1:27, Romans 13:4 and Matthew 5:37 can be indexed as distinct study gates, with their literal KJV contexts preserved.
- **Darkness / Dawn:** Figurative and literary states; separate interpretations from measured light or physical cosmology.
- **D / DD / DDD:** Repetition and satirical initials are separate from language code `ddd` (Dongotono) and source-file names.
- **Death / Door / Dominion / Dimension / Divine / Dante:** Optional research and literary connections, with factual, religious and invented worlds clearly labeled.

## Three-stage player loop

1. **Read:** Display the KJV source reference and the original creator-authored scenario, clearly separated.
2. **Rightly divide:** Ask the player to distinguish literal words, metaphor, historical background, phonetic wordplay, and an unsupported claim. Every interpretation has its own evidence label.
3. **Decide:** Give nonviolent moral/educational choices, document the consequences, and let the player revisit earlier decisions. No coercion, profiling, paywall, or claims to diagnose a person.

**Arithmetic anchors:** `1+1=2` (BUILD), `1-1=0` (CLEAR), protecting the one. `Yes is Yes — No is No.`
