The difference between a word and what someone makes it say

Start with the move you can do with your hands. Write GOD. Read the letters backward. You get DOG. Reverse them again and you are back at GOD. Nathan makes the same move with LIVE / EVIL, LOVE / EVOL, and CAT / TAC. Once the rule is written down, anyone can check the result one letter at a time.

TABOR → ROBAT follows the reversal rule. ROBAT → ROBOT changes one letter and completes Nathan’s wordplay. FREE → FEE removes R. The new word gives the manuscript a question about power. When someone promises a freedom, who can exercise it, what must they pay, and who collects? The wordplay opens the question. The contract, rule, or receipt can answer it.

COME and CUM can sound the same, /kʌm/. With speech, the sentence and the person speaking can settle what the sound alone leaves open. Nathan’s correction, “I picked my nose,” supplies the wording he intended. By keeping the initial transcript and the correction together, a reader can see exactly what he changed. The Nathan Tabor Way, applied to a record

Build · (1+1=2). Bring the statement and its source together so the reader can see the claim and check it.

Integrate. Bring one checked finding into the larger account with its dates, scope, and source still attached.

Verify. Turn the account around. Can someone start with the cited record and arrive at the same statement? If the source leaves a question open, carry that question forward.
