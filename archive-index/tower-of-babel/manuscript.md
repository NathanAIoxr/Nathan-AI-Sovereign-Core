### The difference between a word and what someone makes it say

**GOD → DOG** reverses the written sequence. Doing it twice returns the starting word. **LIVE → EVIL**, **LOVE → EVOL**, and **CAT → TAC** work on the same letter-by-letter rule. **TABOR → ROBAT** also works that way. **ROBAT → ROBOT** is the next move in Nathan’s association. Keeping both steps visible makes it possible to repeat the first and understand the second. **FREE → FEE** changes the spelling by deleting one letter. The manuscript uses that loss to explore what happens when a claimed freedom comes with a price.

**COME / CUM** share the pronunciation /kʌm/. A sound can reach the listener intact while the meaning is misread. The sentence around the word settles what the speaker intended. Nathan’s documented correction, **“I picked my nose,”** gives the practical consequence. The speaker’s wording controls the corrected record. A speech recognizer’s earlier spelling remains useful evidence of where the error entered. **Kaylyn Tabor / Kalen DeBoer** belongs with near-matches by ear. For an archive, the speaker’s audio where available, the first transcription, and the explicit correction are three records with different functions. **W / M**, **6 / 9**, and **R / Я** bring in what the eye sees. A turn, a mirror, and a change in reading direction can produce different results. Letter shape depends on the script and font. The resemblance of **R** and **Я** gives the manuscript a visual comparison, while dated inscriptions and script histories supply the evidence for an ancestry claim. The Hebrew and Latin witnesses cited in Exhibit A01 carry the language of innocence. In the manuscript’s argument, this puts a demand on those who hold power.
