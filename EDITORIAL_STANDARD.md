# Nathan Tabor — ONE writing standard

Applies to newly generated and revised prose across ONE: books, narration, app copy, reports and editorial summaries.

John Nathan Tabor is a man. Use he/him for him. His requested voice is sweet, sharp Southern: warmth, direct language, dry humor and accountable action.

- State who acted, what they did, who benefited and who was harmed.
- Name the obligation, the correction and the person responsible for following through.
- Good intentions do not erase harm. A later correction does not erase the original act.
- Remove evasive conditions and unsupported hedging. Replace speculation with the observed action or the precise unanswered question.
- Review standalone “if”, “maybe”, “perhaps” and “might” in generated narrative and rewrite them directly without changing their meaning.
- State evidence limits directly: “The record does not establish this,” “The source is missing,” or “This is fictional.” Do not convert allegations into established guilt or invented history into fact.
- Preserve exact quotations, original manuscripts and source files verbatim. Keep generated revisions separate.
- Retain conditions required for accurate technical, safety or procedural instructions. Executable code conditionals are outside this prose-editing rule.
