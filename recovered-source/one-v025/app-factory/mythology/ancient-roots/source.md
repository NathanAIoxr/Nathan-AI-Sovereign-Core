# THE ANCIENT DIVINE ROOTS — NORSE, INDIGENOUS AMERICAN, ZOROASTRIAN & SATANIC TRADITIONS

Gods · Goddesses · Spirits · Ancient Languages · Names · Etymologies · Sacred Powers · Symbols · Artifacts

I'll use the same method as our biblical and Greek studies: identify each original name, trace its oldest recoverable linguistic form, explain its meaning, and connect it to the beings, functions and sacred objects associated with it.

A critical distinction: a shared meaning does not always mean a shared linguistic origin or the same deity. Some historical connections are demonstrable; others are theological interpretations or comparable stories.

## I. THE VIKINGS — OLD NORSE AND GERMANIC DIVINE ROOTS

The Norse traditions were practiced across Scandinavia and the wider Germanic world. The Viking Age was approximately 800–1100 CE, but some Germanic divine names are substantially older.

Our principal literary sources include the Poetic Edda and Prose Edda, supplemented by runic inscriptions, place names, archaeology and earlier historical writers.

## II. INDIGENOUS AMERICAN SPIRITUAL TRADITIONS — NAMES AND ROOTS

There is no single Native American pantheon. Hundreds of Indigenous nations have distinct languages, histories, creation accounts, sacred beings and continuing religious practices.

For an ancient-roots study, we can compare publicly documented traditions from North, Central and South America, while keeping each nation's beliefs separate.

### 5. Baphomet — the later occult symbol

Baphomet has a different history from the ancient biblical Satan.

The familiar goat-headed, winged image was developed by Éliphas Lévi in the nineteenth century, combining symbols of opposing forces, balance and esoteric philosophy. It was subsequently adopted or reinterpreted by occult and Satanist movements.

It is not an ancient surviving portrait of Satan or a recognized member of the original Hebrew divine council.

## What this gives us

We now have an initial comparative archive of well over 150 named figures, divine titles, spirits, supernatural beings and ancient terms across these four broad categories, with root-word connections, possessions, cosmological roles and historical distinctions.

For your larger name-and-root system, I would preserve every entry with five attributes: original spelling, earliest known source, literal or proposed etymology, function or attributes, and historically established relationships to other names.
