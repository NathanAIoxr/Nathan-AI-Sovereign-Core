## Full research narrative — supplied text

I'll follow the historical trail through **original texts, early Bible translations, dated inscriptions, and old printed records**, rather than relying on modern interpretations alone.

There are two distinct investigations here: the history of reversed R-shaped letters, and the relationship between **Niger in the Bible, the Latin word *niger*, the English racial slur, and your proposed original Goddess connection**.

One important starting point: **Acts 13:1 (KJV) explicitly names “Simeon that was called Niger.”** That's a real biblical reference, not a modern invention. The Latin *niger* means black or dark.

The question is what the earliest records actually establish about that name, its later linguistic descendants, and any connection to an ancient goddess.

### 1. The R investigation: an old book describing reversed letters

**Historical book: *The Origin of the Alphabet* (1943 edition)**

The text explicitly describes alternating Greek writing directions and says letters in alternating lines were reversed like mirror writing.

Read the digitized historical book

This is significant evidence that the orientation of ancient letters was not always fixed. The book is a later historical study, however, not an eyewitness account from antiquity.

**Older published research: *Man and His Ancestor***

A digitized copy in the University of Illinois collection discusses the ancient Sigean inscription, describes letters facing different directions, and references Pausanias's account of writing on the Chest of Kypselos.

Read the scanned book, page 205

Pausanias is particularly relevant to your request for what people wrote closer to the time of the artifacts: he was an ancient Greek writer, rather than a modern alphabet historian.

These sources establish letter reversal as a historical practice. They do not yet identify an ancient reversed-R inscription that directly precedes Latin R.

### 2. NIGER in the Bible: follow the original wording

**ACTS 13:1 — KING JAMES VERSION, 1611**

> “Simeon that was called Niger”

The verse identifies Niger as an additional name or description of Simeon, a man among the prophets and teachers at Antioch.

The Greek text contains **Νίγερ (Niger)**. This is a Greek rendering of a Latin name or descriptor.

The Latin adjective *niger* means black or dark. Biblical commentary notes that Simeon's designation could have referred to his complexion, possibly indicating African ancestry, but his exact ancestry is not established by the verse.

That is an actual ancient textual connection.

### 3. Trace the word and the additional G

**DOCUMENTED ETYMOLOGICAL LINEAGE**

**NIGER** — Latin — black / dark

**NEGRO / NÈGRE** — Spanish and French developments

**NEGER → NIGGER** — Historical English spellings, including the doubled G

Oxford's dictionary traces the English slur through earlier European forms to Latin *niger*. Historical records include *neger* in 1568, while later English spellings developed the doubled consonant.

So there is a documented connection between the biblical name's Latin source and the later English word.

But **the second G did not get added to the biblical name itself**. It appeared in a later English spelling lineage, through language changes and racial terminology. The modern word is a deeply offensive racial slur, whatever its earlier linguistic ancestry.

### 4. The Goddess connection: an ancient Egyptian text worth examining

This search uncovered a much stronger historical lead than simply comparing modern words.

A scholarly examination of ancient Egyptian religious texts identifies a **first-century BCE papyrus, P. Berlin 15660**, referring to Isis as the great goddess associated with the Black Land. It also examines a Greek magical papyrus invoking Isis in connection with the *Perfect Black*.

**Isis — Ancient Egyptian Goddess**

The documented connections include motherhood, kingship, fertility, the Black Land of Egypt, and religious imagery involving black garments.

Getty Research Institute: ancient Isis inscriptions

Another study, *Dea Africa: Examining the Evidence*, investigates ancient Roman representations of Africa as a female divinity and carefully distinguishes goddess worship from personifications of a geographical region.

That gives us three historically grounded subjects to compare:

| Subject | Ancient evidence | What it establishes |
|---|---|---|
| **Niger** | Acts 13:1 | A man called Simeon Niger |
| **Niger / nigr-** | Latin vocabulary | Black or dark; ancestral source of later European words |
| **Isis and the Black Land** | Egyptian religious papyri | A goddess associated with Egypt and blackness |

**What I have not found:** an ancient document naming a goddess *Niger* and identifying her as the original source of the later English racial slur. Isis is an authentic ancient goddess, but her name does not derive from Latin *niger*.

### 5. What old newspapers add

One particularly useful historical source is the *Century Dictionary* of 1895, which discussed the racial slur's contemporary usage. The historical etymology record preserves its observation that the word was associated with contempt even though usage varied.

A later example is *Harper's Weekly*, June 2, 1906, which discussed Booker T. Washington's preference for capitalizing *Negro*. This documents how naming and racial terminology were publicly debated at the time.

These are leads into historical language usage, not evidence that the slur originally named a goddess.

### The research trail now

The most promising documents are the **ancient inscriptions showing reversed letters**, **Acts 13:1 in Greek and early English**, **P. Berlin 15660 concerning Isis**, and **historical English publications documenting the transition from *neger* to later spellings**.

They support two substantial historical findings: letter orientation genuinely changed in antiquity, and the biblical name *Niger* shares a Latin source with the ancestry of the English slur.

The proposed connection to an **original Goddess** remains a separate hypothesis. The Egyptian papyrus is the strongest ancient lead I found for investigating the goddess-and-blackness connection, but it does not establish that the names are identical or that one became the other.
