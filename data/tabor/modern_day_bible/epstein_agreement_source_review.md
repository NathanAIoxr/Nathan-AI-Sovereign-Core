**Read the deal from the children’s side.** Girls reported sexual abuse. Federal prosecutors drafted an indictment, then agreed behind closed doors to drop the federal case in South Florida. They made that decision without consulting the victims and kept them in the dark after signing. The agreement also promised that the South Florida office would forgo federal prosecution of four named people and any potential co-conspirators. That was a betrayal of the children who came forward. [DOJ review](https://www.justice.gov/d9/press-releases/attachments/2020/11/12/epstein._executive_summary.pdf) · [District court opinion](https://www.law.cornell.edu/sites/default/files/women-and-justice/Epstein-CASE-NO-08-80736-CIV-MARRA.pdf) · [Eleventh Circuit, In re Wild](https://media.ca11.uscourts.gov/opinions/pub/files/201913843.enb.pdf)

- **Who made the deal?** The federal agreement was signed on **September 24, 2007**, during George W. Bush’s presidency. Bush nominated Alexander Acosta to be U.S. Attorney for the Southern District of Florida. The DOJ review identifies **Acosta as the official who approved the agreement**. The records I reviewed establish Bush’s role in the nomination and Acosta’s role in the deal. [DOJ review](https://www.justice.gov/d9/press-releases/attachments/2020/11/12/epstein._executive_summary.pdf) · [Bush White House nomination archive](https://georgewbush-whitehouse.archives.gov/news/nominations/4.html)
- **What was secret?** Epstein’s **June 30, 2008** Florida guilty plea was public. The federal agreement behind it was filed under seal. Epstein was sentenced to **18 months**, served **less than 13**, and was allowed out for work release for up to **12 hours a day**. The *Palm Beach Post* reported the agreement’s terms after it was unsealed in **2009**. Julie K. Brown’s *Miami Herald* investigation brought it to national attention in **2018**. [DOJ review](https://www.justice.gov/d9/press-releases/attachments/2020/11/12/epstein._executive_summary.pdf) · [Palm Beach Post clipping in court exhibit](https://media-cdn.rollcall.com/epstein-files/doe-v-us-908-cv-80736-sdfl-2008-247-02.pdf) · [Miami Herald series](https://www.miamiherald.com/news/local/article238237729.html)
- **What did the courts decide?** In **2019**, Judge Kenneth Marra found that prosecutors violated victims’ rights under the Crime Victims’ Rights Act. In **2021**, the Eleventh Circuit held that the law gave Courtney Wild no right to enforce those rights through a stand-alone lawsuit without an existing federal criminal proceeding. The appeals court also said prosecutors should have consulted her and the other victims. [District court opinion](https://www.law.cornell.edu/sites/default/files/women-and-justice/Epstein-CASE-NO-08-80736-CIV-MARRA.pdf) · [Eleventh Circuit, In re Wild](https://media.ca11.uscourts.gov/opinions/pub/files/201913843.enb.pdf)

**More than 1,000 victims.** That’s what the DOJ and FBI said their broader review confirmed in July 2025. “Dozens” described the scope alleged in the **2019 New York indictment**, which covered conduct from **2002 through 2005**. The broader memo gives no age breakdown or exact total. The public record supports **more than 1,000** and leaves the full number unknown.
