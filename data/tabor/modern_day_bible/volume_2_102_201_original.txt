102–121: Prophetic Shock-Art, Nudity & Body Horror

102. Rubbing dung on faces at the altar (Malachi 2:3): God threatens corrupt priests that he will smear animal excrement ("the dung of your solemn feasts" in KJV) directly onto their faces.
103. Walking around stripped and howling like a jackal (Micah 1:8): The prophet Micah announces he will go stripped and naked, wailing like jackals and moaning like ostriches.
104. David stripping naked in front of servant girls (2 Samuel 6:14, 20): King David dances so hard before the Ark in a tiny linen ephod that his wife Michal mocks him for exposing himself to the slave women.
105. Saul lying naked in a prophetic trance all day (1 Samuel 19:24): King Saul strips off all his clothes and lies completely naked on the ground for a full day and night.
106. Peter fishing butt-naked (John 21:7): When Simon Peter realizes Jesus is on the shore, he has to put his outer garment on because he was working completely naked on the boat.
107. A disciple running away naked (Mark 14:51–52): When soldiers grab a young man following Jesus by his linen sheet, he slips out of it and sprints away stark naked.
108. Noah passing out drunk and naked in his tent (Genesis 9:21–24): Noah gets wasted on his own wine, passes out naked, and curses his grandson Canaan after Ham sees him exposed.
109. Sleeping in a cow-manure pile (Jeremiah 48:26): God declares Moab will get so drunk he will wallow in his own vomit ("wallow in his vomit" in KJV) and become a laughingstock.
110. Dogs licking up vomit as a proverb (Proverbs 26:11 / 2 Peter 2:22): Scripture explicitly compares a fool repeating mistakes to a dog returning to eat its own vomit.
111. Lying paralyzed on your left side for 390 days (Ezekiel 4:4–5): God forces Ezekiel to lie tied up on his left side for over a year without turning over to symbolize Israel's sin.
112. Eating your own arm flesh from hunger (Isaiah 9:20): During total societal collapse, people are described as so starved they chew the flesh off their own arms.
113. Boils and scabs on the vagina/secret parts (Isaiah 3:17 KJV): In the King James Version, God punishes haughty women by smiting their heads with scabs and "discovering their secret parts."
114. Pissing against a wall (1 Samuel 25:22, 34 KJV): The King James Bible repeatedly uses the literal phrase "any that pisseth against the wall" to mean every single male in a household.
115. Men eating their own dung and drinking piss—part two (2 Kings 18:27 KJV): The KJV literally translates Rabshakeh's threat as men eating their own dung and drinking "their own piss."
116. A king’s bowels rotting out over two years (2 Chronicles 21:18–19): King Jehoram is struck with an incurable intestinal disease until his bowels literally fall out of his body.
117. Rotting flesh while still standing on your feet (Zechariah 14:12): God promises a plague where enemies' flesh rots off their bones, their eyes melt in their sockets, and their tongues rot in their mouths while standing up.
118. Leprosy turning skin white as snow (Numbers 12:10): God strikes Miriam with instant leprosy until her skin flakes like snow because she criticized Moses's marriage.
119. Bleeding nonstop for twelve years (Mark 5:25): A woman suffers a continuous vaginal hemorrhage for twelve straight years, making her permanently untouchable under Mosaic law.
120. Cutting yourself with stones until bleeding (Mark 5:5): A demon-possessed man lives naked in tombs, screaming night and day while slashing his own body with sharp rocks.
121. Eunuchs making themselves castrated for heaven (Matthew 19:12): Jesus mentions men who "have made themselves eunuchs for the kingdom of heaven's sake."

122–141: Extreme Torture, Executions & War Crimes

122. Sawing prophets in half (Hebrews 11:37): Faithful martyrs are listed as being tortured, stoned, and literally "sawn asunder" (cut in two with a saw).
123. Burning people alive in a brick furnace (Daniel 3:19–22): Nebuchadnezzar heats a furnace seven times hotter than normal—so hot the flames incinerate the guards throwing prisoners inside.
124. Roasting false prophets alive in fire (Jeremiah 29:22): Two adulterous prophets, Zedekiah and Ahab, are literally roasted in fire by the King of Babylon.
125. Impaling corpses on trees until sunset (Joshua 10:26): Joshua executes five captive kings and hangs their bodies on five trees until evening.
126. Breaking legs with iron clubs on a cross (John 19:31–32): Roman soldiers smash the shinbones of crucified men with heavy mallets to suffocate them faster.
127. Scourging with bone-tipped whips (John 19:1): Pilate orders Jesus flogged with a Roman flagrum designed to shred skin down to the ribs.
128. Stoning an entire family and their livestock to death (Joshua 7:24–25): Because Achan stole banned loot, Israel stones him, his sons, his daughters, his oxen, his donkeys, and his sheep—and burns them all.
129. The earth swallowing families alive (Numbers 16:31–33): The ground splits open beneath Korah, Dathan, and Abiram, swallowing their wives, kids, and toddlers alive into the pit.
130. Fire burning 250 priests alive (Numbers 16:35): Right after the earth swallows the rebels, fire shoots out from God and incinerates 250 leaders holding incense censers.
131. Burning two priests alive for using the wrong fire (Leviticus 10:1–2): Aaron’s sons Nadab and Abihu offer unauthorized incense fire, and God torches them dead on the temple floor.
132. Forcing a father not to cry at his sons' corpses (Leviticus 10:6): Right after God burns Nadab and Abihu alive, Moses forbids Aaron from tearing his clothes or mourning them on pain of death.
133. Slicing a bull into pieces and mailing it as a draft notice (1 Samuel 11:7): King Saul chops up a yoke of oxen and sends bloody pieces across Israel with a threat to do the same to anyone's cattle who skips war.
134. Hacking a captive king to pieces before the altar (1 Samuel 15:33): The prophet Samuel takes a sword and personally hacks King Agag into pieces in front of the Lord.
135. Beheading a sleeping general with his own sword (Judith / Jael parallel — 1 Samuel 17:51): Teenage David stands over Goliath, pulls out the giant’s own massive sword, and saws off his head.
136. Dragging a severed head around as a trophy (1 Samuel 17:54): David carries Goliath’s severed head all the way to Jerusalem and keeps his armor in his tent.
137. Serving a prophet’s severed head on a dinner platter (Mark 6:25–28): Herodias’s daughter dances at a birthday party and demands John the Baptist's head brought out immediately on a serving tray.
138. Driving a spear through an interracial couple mid-sex (Numbers 25:7–8): Phinehas follows an Israelite man and a Midianite woman into a tent and impales both of them through the belly with one thrust of his spear.
139. Impaling seven innocent grandsons to end a famine (2 Samuel 21:6–9): King David hands over seven of Saul's sons to be executed and hung on a hill to appease God during a drought.
140. A mother guarding rotting corpses from vultures for months (2 Samuel 21:10): Rizpah spreads sackcloth on a rock and fights off wild birds and beasts from devouring her dead sons' corpses from harvest until rain falls.
141. Throwing 10,000 prisoners of war off a cliff (2 Chronicles 25:12): Judah’s army captures 10,000 Edomites alive, marches them to the top of a rock cliff, and throws them all off to smash on the rocks below.

142–161: Twisted Family Dynamics, Sex & Taboos

142. David marrying a 14-year-old virgin to keep him warm (1 Kings 1:1–4): When old King David can't stay warm, servants search the country for a young virgin, Abishag, to lie in his bed—though he doesn't have sex with her.
143. Executing a brother for asking to marry his dad’s bed-warmer (1 Kings 2:17–25): King Solomon has his own half-brother Adonijah assassinated simply for requesting to marry Abishag.
144. Sleeping with your servant because you're infertile (Genesis 16:2–4): Sarah tells Abraham to sleep with her Egyptian slave Hagar to get pregnant, then abuses Hagar the second she conceives.
145. Two sisters using their maids as baby-making surrogates (Genesis 30:3–13): Rachel and Leah both throw their female slaves into Jacob’s bed to rack up higher child counts against each other.
146. Isaac lying that his wife is his sister—just like his dad did (Genesis 26:7): Following Abraham's exact playbook, Isaac tells local men Rebekah is his sister so they won't kill him to take her.
147. Boaz waking up with a woman uncovered at his feet (Ruth 3:7–8): Ruth sneaks onto the threshing floor at midnight, uncovers Boaz’s "feet" (a classic Hebrew euphemism for genitals) while he's drunk and asleep, and lies down.
148. A brother murdering his half-brother over sister-rape (2 Samuel 13:28–29): Absalom waits two full years after Amnon rapes Tamar, gets him drunk at a sheepshearing party, and has servants slaughter him.
149. A father refusing to punish his rapist son because he loved him (2 Samuel 13:21): King David hears Amnon raped his daughter Tamar, gets furious, and does absolutely nothing to punish him.
150. A husband forced to watch his wife raped by another man (Deuteronomy 28:30): Listed as a covenant curse: "Thou shalt betroth a wife, and another man shall lie with her."
151. Soldiers commanded to kill every baby boy and non-virgin woman (Numbers 31:17): Moses gets angry that officers spared women and children, ordering them to execute every male child and every woman who has slept with a man.
152. Giving away your daughter to whoever conquers a town (Joshua 15:16): Caleb offers his daughter Achsah as a marriage prize to whatever warrior captures Kiriath Sepher.
153. Samson’s wedding riddle bet over 30 outfits (Judges 14:12–19): After his bride leaks his riddle answer, Samson murders 30 innocent men in Ashkelon and strips their clothes to pay his gambling debt.
154. Giving a man’s bride to his best man (Judges 14:20): While Samson storms off angry from his own wedding, his father-in-law gives Samson’s bride to the best man instead.
155. Samson hiring a prostitute in Gaza (Judges 16:1): God's chosen superhuman judge casually visits a prostitute in enemy territory before ripping the city gates off their hinges.
156. Rahab the prostitute hid spies and got into Jesus’s bloodline (Joshua 2:1 / Matthew 1:5): One of the most celebrated heroes of faith in the New Testament was literally a Jericho brothel-keeper.
157. Tamar pretending to be a prostitute also enters Jesus’s bloodline (Genesis 38 / Matthew 1:3): Jesus's official genealogy in Matthew directly lists Tamar—who seduced her father-in-law disguised as a hooker.
158. Bathsheba listed in Jesus’s genealogy via adultery and murder (Matthew 1:6): Matthew explicitly highlights that Solomon was born to "her that had been the wife of Urias."
159. The Whore of Babylon drunk on martyr blood (Revelation 17:4–6): Revelation describes a flashy prostitute riding a seven-headed beast, holding a gold cup overflowing with filth and blood.
160. God commanding Hosea to buy back his cheating wife (Hosea 3:1–2): After Hosea’s wife leaves him for other lovers, God tells him to pay fifteen shekels of silver and barley to buy her back from another man.
161. Women weeping for a pagan fertility god at the temple gate (Ezekiel 8:14): Ezekiel sees Israelite women sitting right inside God's temple weeping over the Babylonian sex/fertility deity Tammuz.

162–181: Unhinged Laws, Slaves & Weird Prohibitions

162. Never plow with an ox and a donkey together (Deuteronomy 22:10): Harnessing two different species to the same plow is strictly outlawed.
163. Never plant two different kinds of seeds in one vineyard (Deuteronomy 22:9): Mixing crop seeds forfeits the entire harvest to the sanctuary.
164. Never breed two different kinds of livestock together (Leviticus 19:19): Cross-breeding animals (like making mules) is banned right alongside wearing polyester-cotton blends.
165. Leave fallen grapes on the ground or face curse rules (Leviticus 19:10): Picking up grapes that drop during harvest is prohibited.
166. A woman is twice as "unclean" after birthing a girl (Leviticus 12:2–5): Giving birth to a boy makes a mother ritually unclean for 7 days plus 33 days of purification; birthing a girl doubles it to 14 days plus 66 days.
167. Buying foreign slaves as permanent inherited property (Leviticus 25:44–46): Israelites are explicitly told they may buy slaves from surrounding nations and pass them down to their children forever.
168. A slave’s wife and kids belong to the master if the slave goes free (Exodus 21:4): If a master gives a Hebrew slave a wife and she has children, the slave leaves alone after six years while the master keeps the wife and kids.
169. Knocking out a slave’s tooth sets them free (Exodus 21:26–27): If an owner hits a slave and knocks out an eye or a single tooth, the slave must be released as compensation.
170. Putting a bird’s nest raid in the Ten Commandments section (Deuteronomy 22:6–7): You can take baby birds or eggs from a nest to eat, as long as you shoo the mother bird away first.
171. Build a railing on your roof or bear bloodguilt (Deuteronomy 22:8): Building a flat-roofed house without a parapet wall makes you legally guilty of murder if a guest falls off.
172. Wearing women's clothes or men's clothes is an abomination (Deuteronomy 22:5): Cross-dressing uses the exact same Hebrew word (toevah) as eating shrimp or worshipping idols.
173. Men with long hair are a disgrace (1 Corinthians 11:14): Paul declares that nature itself teaches that long hair on a man is shameful—despite Samson and Nazarites being forbidden to cut theirs.
174. Women must keep silent in church and ask husbands at home (1 Corinthians 14:34–35): Paul calls it "shameful" for a woman to speak inside a church gathering.
175. Women are saved through childbearing (1 Timothy 2:15): Paul writes that women are saved through having babies, provided they continue in faith and modesty.
176. No gold, pearls, braided hair, or expensive clothes for women (1 Timothy 2:9): Fundamentalist women wearing wedding rings, pearls, or braided hairstyles directly violate Paul's explicit dress code.
177. Greet every church member with a holy kiss (Romans 16:16 / 1 Peter 5:14): Repeated five times in the New Testament, believers are commanded to kiss each other in church.
178. Handling venomous snakes and drinking poison proves faith (Mark 16:18): The KJV ending of Mark claims true believers will pick up deadly serpents and drink lethal poison without dying.
179. Anointing sick people with olive oil instead of medicine (James 5:14): Church elders are instructed to rub oil on sick members and pray rather than relying on doctors.
180. Gouging out your eye or chopping off your hand to avoid hell (Matthew 5:29–30): Jesus says it is better to rip out your own eyeball or amputate your right hand than go to hell whole.
181. Hating your own parents, spouse, and kids to follow Jesus (Luke 14:26): Jesus states that no one can be his disciple unless they "hate" their father, mother, wife, children, brothers, sisters, and own life.

188. Sweaty handkerchiefs and aprons curing diseases (Acts 19:11–12): Pieces of cloth that touched Paul’s sweaty skin are carried away to heal sick people and cast out demons.
189. God striking a couple dead for holding back cash from a church offering (Acts 5:1–10): Ananias and Sapphira lie about how much money they got from selling land, and God drops both of them dead at Peter's feet.
190. Paul blinding a sorcerer on the spot (Acts 13:8–11): Paul stares down Elymas the magician and strikes him completely blind in front of a Roman governor.
191. Seven sons of a priest beaten naked by a demon-possessed man (Acts 19:14–16): Seven Jewish exorcists try to cast out a demon using Jesus’s name; the possessed man jumps all seven of them, rips their clothes off, and beats them bloody.
192. A slave girl making cash off a fortune-telling demon (Acts 16:16–18): A girl possessed by a spirit of divination (literally a "python spirit" in Greek) earns huge profits for her owners until Paul evicts it.

194. Satan asking God for permission to ruin Job’s life (Job 1:6–12): God boasts about Job's loyalty to Satan and gives Satan the green light to slaughter Job's kids, burn his servants, and cover him in boils.

196. Locusts with human faces, women’s hair, lion teeth, and scorpion tails (Revelation 9:7–10): Monsters emerge from a smoke pit to torture unsealed humans for five months—so painful people beg to die but can't.
197. A 200-million-man army on fire-breathing lion-headed horses (Revelation 9:16–17): Cavalry mounts with heads like lions spew fire, smoke, and sulfur out of their mouths, killing a third of humanity.
198. Eating a bitter scroll that tastes like honey (Revelation 10:9–10): An angel forces John to eat a small book that tastes sweet in his mouth but turns his stomach violently sour.
199. Blood rising as high as horses' bridles for 180 miles (Revelation 14:20): God's winepress of wrath crushes people until a river of blood flows 1,600 stadia deep enough to reach a horse's bit.
200. Birds invited to eat the flesh of kings, generals, and horses (Revelation 19:17–18): An angel screams for every vulture in the sky to gather for "the great supper of God" to feast on human corpses.
201. Jesus returning with a blood-soaked robe and a sword coming out of his mouth (Revelation 19:13–15): Far from gentle Sunday-school paintings, Jesus rides a white horse wearing clothes dipped in blood with a sharp sword protruding from his mouth to strike down nations.
