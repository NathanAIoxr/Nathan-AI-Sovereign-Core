The KJV uses **H7854** for a human opponent in 1 Kings 11:14, where Hadad is called “an adversary.” In Job 1:8–9 the adversary questions Job’s motives before God. In 2:3 God describes the harm as “without cause,” and in 2:6–7 God sets the limit and the adversary strikes Job. Tabor’s **“007 AGENT”** describes his reading of that role.

In 13:3 Job asks to reason with God. In 19:6–7 he says God overthrew him and says he cries out without receiving judgment. In 31:35 he calls for the Almighty to answer. In 42:7 God says Job spoke rightly of him where his friends failed. In 42:11 the narrator attributes the calamity to the Lord, and 42:12 records the blessing.

**Tabor’s reading:** “HA SATAN is Job” identifies the adversarial role Job takes when he brings his case against God. Job 1:8 uses the Hebrew title for the figure God addresses about Job. Job’s later speeches supply the evidence for Tabor’s reading of the role reversal. Tabor’s earlier *52 Consciousness* notes emphasize “without cause,” and his JNT rendering of Job 42:7 reads “NOT today GOD, NOT ever AGAIN.” Prior chat and correspondence corroborated the earlier work. | שָׂטָן (*śāṭān*) | [H7854](https://biblehub.com/hebrew/7854.htm) | Adversary or opponent. Strong’s links the noun to H7853. In Job the article gives “the adversary.” The KJV uses “an adversary” for the angel of the LORD in Numbers 22:22 and for Hadad in 1 Kings 11:14. |
| שִׂטְנָה (*śiṭnāh*) | [H7855](https://biblehub.com/hebrew/7855.htm) | Accusation, opposition, or hostility. A related noun from H7853, used in Ezra 4:6 for an accusation. |
| אִיּוֹב (*ʾIyyōḇ*) and אָיַב (*ʾāyaḇ*) | [H347](https://biblehub.com/hebrew/347.htm), [H340](https://biblehub.com/hebrew/340.htm) | H347 is the personal name Job.
